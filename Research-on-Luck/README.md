# Research on Luck

Research on Luck is one continuing interdisciplinary research project organized into distinct research phases. Each phase preserves its own questions, concepts, evidence, and limits so that later work can build selectively on earlier work without rewriting its intellectual history.

## Phase I

[Phase I](Phase-I/README.md) is the completed first research cycle. It developed a bounded account of luck centered on consequential uncertainty and on luck attribution as a relational, ex post evaluation. The cycle distinguishes system, environment, interaction, and uncertainty; separates opportunity stages and capability from outcome; and examines selection, feedback, and trajectories across several empirical domains.

Phase I also establishes an empirical identification boundary. Its cases and validation studies support a coherent conceptual and measurement architecture, but they do not establish universal causal weights, population prevalence, reliable prediction, or a single scale-independent measure of luck. The absence of a public denominator containing both recognized and unrecognized opportunities stops the completed cycle short of the proposed population-level identification strategy.

## Phase II

Phase II is the active continuation of the project. It may reuse, reinterpret, place downstream, or retire selected Phase I constructs, but it does not retroactively revise Phase I. Current Phase II materials include the [Conceptual Checkpoint II](Research-on-Luck-Phase-II-Conceptual-Checkpoint-II.md) and subsequent research development.
