# General Theory

## The Evolving Theoretical Core of the Research on Luck Project

---

# Overview

The **General Theory** directory represents the evolving theoretical core of the **Research on Luck** project.

Unlike the preceding directories, which establish conceptual foundations, review existing scholarship, develop formal models, construct empirical methodologies, and evaluate the framework across multiple domains, this directory integrates those developments into progressively more general theoretical propositions.

The objective is not to summarize the repository, but to distill the broader scientific principles that emerge from it.

Accordingly, the General Theory is expected to evolve continuously as additional empirical evidence accumulates.

---

# Why a General Theory?

Scientific theories should emerge from evidence rather than precede it.

The preceding sections of this repository were intentionally organized to support this process.

* **Foundations** define concepts.
* **Literature** synthesizes existing knowledge.
* **Formalization** develops explanatory mechanisms.
* **Measurement** establishes empirical methodology.
* **Case Studies** evaluate the framework across multiple domains.

Only after these stages can broader theoretical principles be proposed with confidence.

The General Theory therefore represents the current state of theoretical understanding rather than a final conclusion.

---

# Scientific Philosophy

This directory follows several guiding principles.

First,

general theory should emerge inductively from systematic comparison across multiple domains rather than from isolated examples.

Second,

theoretical propositions should remain open to revision as new evidence becomes available.

Third,

scientific progress is viewed as an iterative interaction between theory and empirical observation.

Accordingly,

the General Theory is intentionally treated as a living component of the research program.

# Relationship to Case Studies

The Case Studies directory provides the primary source of empirical refinement.

Each domain contributes evidence regarding:

* recurring mechanisms,
* theoretical consistency,
* competing explanations,
* and previously unrecognized phenomena.

Accordingly,

discoveries emerging from comparative case analysis should inform future revisions of the General Theory.

The relationship is therefore bidirectional.

Theory guides empirical investigation.

Empirical investigation refines theory.

---

# Guiding Principles

Documents in this directory should:

* distinguish established findings from emerging hypotheses;
* clearly identify the empirical basis supporting each proposition;
* remain consistent with earlier conceptual and formal work;
* acknowledge uncertainty where evidence remains limited;
* encourage theoretical refinement rather than premature closure;
* distinguish scientific inference from speculation.

The objective is to develop increasingly general explanations while remaining grounded in empirical evidence.

---

# Versioning

The General Theory should be viewed as an evolving scientific framework.

Future revisions are expected as additional domains are investigated.

Changes should be driven by:

* new empirical evidence,
* comparative analyses,
* theoretical refinement,
* methodological advances,
* and constructive criticism.

Earlier versions should remain documented whenever significant theoretical revisions occur.

Scientific progress depends upon preserving the history of theoretical development as well as its current state.

---

# Long-Term Goal

The long-term goal of the **General Theory** directory is to develop an increasingly unified scientific theory explaining how stochastic events interact with opportunity, dynamic systems, institutions, and networks to shape trajectories across multiple domains of human activity.

Whether such a unified theory ultimately emerges remains an empirical question rather than an assumption.

Accordingly, this directory records the current theoretical understanding of the Research on Luck project while remaining open to continual refinement as new evidence accumulates.

Its purpose is therefore not to present a final theory, but to document the ongoing evolution of one.
