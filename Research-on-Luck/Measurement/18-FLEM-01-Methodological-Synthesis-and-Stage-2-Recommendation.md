# FLEM-01 Methodological Synthesis and Stage 2 Recommendation

## Status

**Completed — 20 July 2026**

This document closes the methodological-learning phase of FLEM-01. It synthesizes the frozen Pass A and Pass B records, evaluates what the episode demonstrates about the recognition instrument, identifies procedural costs and failure risks, and defines the next validation step.

It does not revise any frozen packet, audit, coder record, agreement report, or adjudicated code.

## Validation Target

FLEM-01 was the first bounded episode under [Validation Study 1](16-Validation-Study-Recognition-Without-Realization.md). The study asks whether potential, access, recognition, pursuit, realization, and foreclosure can be distinguished using contemporaneous evidence without projecting later outcomes backward.

The episode tested four candidate opportunities associated with Fleming's 1929 paper and then followed their trajectories through three later checkpoints. The design also tested whether actor and system attribution could remain separate as realization moved from Fleming and St Mary's to Oxford clinical and research systems and finally to the United States wartime production network.

## Evidence Produced

### Pass A

The two independent Pass A records produced:

- 23/24 stage-code agreement;
- 3/4 final-category agreement;
- complete agreement on the focal-system boundary and recognition codes; and
- one substantive instrument problem involving the 01C realization-timing rule and overlap between Categories 5 and 6.

The [Pass A agreement and adjudication report](../References/06-Fleming-Penicillin-Pass-A-Agreement-and-Adjudication.md) converted that disagreement into prospective rules rather than rewriting the locked records.

### Pass B

Pass B used fixed candidates, sequential evidence release, candidate-specific realization thresholds, component coding for the conjunctive 01D candidate, explicit actor boundaries, and a frozen primary-source bundle.

The two independent Pass B records produced:

- 12/12 candidate-checkpoint classification agreement;
- 72/72 stage-code agreement;
- agreement that 01C reached realization at B2;
- agreement that 01D reached realization only at B3 after operational production scale was supplied; and
- agreement that later collective realization did not enlarge Fleming's original recognition target.

The [Pass B agreement and adjudication report](../References/21-Fleming-Pass-B-Agreement-and-Adjudication.md) documents the complete comparison.

## What FLEM-01 Supports

Within this single episode, the evidence provisionally supports five methodological conclusions.

1. **Candidate-specific recognition can be distinguished from encounter and broad retrospective attribution.** Fleming's recognition of antibacterial properties and specified uses did not become recognition of every later therapeutic and production possibility.
2. **Recognition, pursuit, and realization can be coded separately.** The 01C and 01D trajectories changed at different checkpoints without requiring retrospective revision of the original recognition target.
3. **Actor and system attribution can change across a trajectory.** Different systems supplied different realization components, and final realization did not belong to one actor.
4. **Conjunctive opportunities require component tests.** Partial achievement of stability, purification, systemic administration, therapeutic effect, or operational scale did not establish the bundled 01D outcome.
5. **Sequential evidence release can control hindsight-sensitive coding.** Later outcome evidence did not cause either coder to enlarge the Pass A recognition boundary.

These findings provisionally support the usability and discriminant validity of the instrument in FLEM-01. They do not validate Proposition 4, estimate a recognition effect, establish prevalence, or demonstrate cross-case or cross-domain portability.

## What FLEM-01 Does Not Establish

FLEM-01 does not show that:

- recognition generally causes pursuit or realization;
- the nine recognition categories are reliable across other episodes;
- false or premature recognition can be identified without hindsight;
- the same realization rules transfer across domains;
- high Pass B agreement would recur with a less structured evidence base;
- or the broader General Theory is empirically validated.

The episode is also selected, historically prominent, source-rich, and nonrepresentative. Agreement may partly reflect the unusually explicit frozen rules developed after Pass A.

## Procedural Assessment

### Controls that added material value

- freezing the candidate definitions before outcome coding;
- separating pre-outcome and outcome-bearing evidence;
- defining realization thresholds and horizons prospectively;
- preserving independent coder records before comparison;
- requiring actor and system attribution;
- using component-level coding for 01D;
- recording all revisions and deviations; and
- preserving disagreement rather than forcing retrospective uniformity.

### Controls that should not become routine

- separate RC1 and RC2 audit cycles for every episode;
- multiple independent reviewer threads;
- reviewer decisioning on nonmaterial wording differences;
- three Pass B checkpoints when the trajectory does not require them;
- and a separate freeze record for every routine replication packet.

FLEM-01 served simultaneously as an episode test and as infrastructure development. Repeating its full governance burden would add cost without proportionate validity gains.

## Prospective Review Architecture

Future episodes use three review tiers.

| Tier | Use | Required controls |
|---|---|---|
| Tier 1 — Full validation | First use of a new instrument or a materially ambiguous episode | Two independent coders plus independent pre-release audit |
| Tier 2 — Controlled replication | Subsequent bounded episodes using Version 0.2 | Coordinator preflight and freeze, two independent coders, disagreement-only adjudication, conditional audit |
| Tier 3 — Larger empirical study | A within-domain population of episodes | Double-coded reliability subsample, exception review, and aggregate validity tests |

FLEM-01 was Tier 1. The next episode should ordinarily be Tier 2.

## Independent-Audit Triggers

An independent audit is required only when at least one material trigger applies:

- a candidate definition changed after evidence collection began;
- the realization criterion or horizon remains contestable;
- the focal actor or system boundary is materially disputed;
- retrospective evidence carries a central ex ante inference;
- outcome leakage cannot be confidently excluded;
- a conjunctive candidate contains unresolved component definitions;
- a coder reports substantive unauthorized exposure;
- the coders disagree on a material stage, actor boundary, or final classification;
- the evidence builder cannot resolve a documented source conflict under the protocol; or
- the result would support promotion of a major theoretical claim.

Minor terminology, formatting, or confidence-wording differences do not trigger independent review unless they change a substantive conclusion.

## Version 0.2 Rule Changes

The prospective protocol and instrument should:

1. retain two independent coders for benchmark episodes;
2. replace routine independent packet audits with a standardized coordinator preflight;
3. use only the number of outcome checkpoints required by the specified trajectory;
4. restrict component codes to the standard evidence statuses, with partiality recorded as a qualifier rather than a separate code;
5. define realization thresholds, horizons, and any scale criterion before packet freeze;
6. report threshold-realization confidence separately from causal-effect confidence;
7. pre-register procedural-exposure and contamination dispositions;
8. use automated or fixed-matrix comparison before adjudication;
9. adjudicate only substantive disagreements; and
10. preserve an immutable record suite with stable numbered filenames and verified links.

## Next-Episode Recommendation

**Proceed to Cold Fusion as the next controlled replication episode after Version 0.2 is frozen.**

Cold Fusion provides a discriminating contrast with Fleming:

- it tests premature or false recognition rather than delayed multi-system realization;
- it contains explicit replication and corrective-feedback stages;
- it remains within Science, reducing some domain variation;
- and it can test whether the instrument distinguishes recognition from validation without treating later rejection as proof that no recognition occurred.

The episode should begin with a bounded feasibility assessment. If contemporary information states, candidate definitions, or replication cutoffs cannot be reconstructed without outcome leakage, the episode should stop or be marked indeterminate rather than forced through the template.

Unless the feasibility assessment activates a Tier 1 trigger, Cold Fusion should use:

1. one pre-outcome Pass A packet;
2. one full-trajectory Pass B packet, with an additional checkpoint only if methodologically necessary;
3. coordinator preflight and freeze;
4. two independent coders;
5. fixed-matrix agreement comparison;
6. disagreement-only adjudication; and
7. conditional independent audit.

## Stage 2 Decision

FLEM-01 alone is insufficient to advance to the within-domain Stage 2 study. It supplies evidence that the instrument can work in one delayed-realization episode, but the construct still requires a contrasting test involving premature or false recognition.

After Cold Fusion, the project should reassess:

- whether recognition remains distinguishable from access, pursuit, validation, and realization;
- whether the coding rules reduce rather than relocate ambiguity;
- whether hindsight-sensitive revisions remain bounded;
- whether the lean Tier 2 process preserves validity;
- and whether the remaining Betamax, Microsoft, and Kodak pilot cases are all necessary before a within-domain design.

## Closure

FLEM-01 is methodologically closed. Its frozen records remain unchanged, its instrument lessons are incorporated prospectively into Version 0.2, and its appropriate contribution is provisional construct validation—not validation of Proposition 4 or the General Theory.

The next gain should come from a contrasting controlled replication, not from expanding the project architecture.
