# Recognition-Episode Coding and Source Plan

## Status and Purpose

**Template Version 0.1**

This document operationalizes [Validation Study 1](16-Validation-Study-Recognition-Without-Realization.md). It is a reusable instrument for constructing opportunity episodes, collecting sources, conducting two-pass coding, and preserving disagreements.

It does not determine whether recognition causes pursuit or realization. It tests whether recognition can be identified independently of those later stages and of outcome hindsight.

## Analytical Boundaries

An episode is a bounded relationship between a focal system and a candidate possibility during a stated interval. Before collecting outcome evidence, the researcher must define:

- the focal system and nested levels;
- the candidate opportunity;
- the environment and relevant interaction;
- the opening, decision, and information-cutoff dates;
- the action or consequence that would count as realization;
- and the horizon at which realization or foreclosure would be evaluated.

Individual recognition is not organizational recognition. Observation is not necessarily recognition, recognition is not authority, and activity is not necessarily committed pursuit.

## Stage Coding Form

Use **supported**, **not supported**, **contested**, **not yet observable**, or **indeterminate**. “Not supported” means the assembled packet lacks adequate affirmative evidence; it does not prove absence.

Also record response or non-response, perceived uncertainty, authority, resources, constraints, and any unrealized alternatives. These are explanatory fields, not substitutes for stage codes.

## Recognition Test

Recognition requires evidence of interpretation, not merely encounter. The coding statement should identify:

1. **who** interpreted the possibility;
2. **what** possibility they perceived;
3. **for what prospective use or consequence**;
4. **when** the interpretation occurred;
5. **what evidence makes the inference contemporaneously recoverable**.

Do not enlarge a narrow recognition claim using later outcomes. Recognition of a laboratory reagent, for example, does not establish recognition of a systemic therapy.

## Source Hierarchy

Preference is not automatic credibility. Source relevance and limitations remain claim-specific.

1. contemporaneous primary records;
2. dated technical, organizational, or institutional records;
3. near-contemporaneous testimony with corroboration;
4. scholarly historical reconstruction;
5. retrospective memoir or interview;
6. later institutional or popular narrative.

A later source may locate archives or expose disagreement but should not establish an earlier information state without corroboration.

## Source-Collection Procedure

1. Fix the episode, cutoff, and realization criterion.
2. Search first for primary records created before the cutoff.
3. Search archival catalogs and scholarly reconstructions for missing or disputed records.
4. Log sources before interpreting their collective meaning.
5. Extract support for each stage and for the strongest alternative coding.
6. Build Pass A using only information available by the cutoff.
7. quarantine later development, outcome, and retrospective celebration for Pass B.
8. Record gaps; do not fill them through plausible narrative.
9. Have a second researcher check the packet for hidden outcome cues.
10. Freeze and version the packet before independent coding.

## Collection Stop Rules

A preliminary packet may close when:

- each positive stage code has at least one directly relevant source;
- reasonable searches have been made for disconfirming evidence;
- the information cutoff and outcome leakage have been independently checked;
- additional sources repeat existing claims without materially changing confidence;
- and unresolved gaps are recorded explicitly.

Collection must continue or the episode must be marked indeterminate when system level, date, candidate opportunity, or the basis for recognition cannot be established.

## Two-Pass Design

### Pass A — Pre-outcome

Coders receive sources available through the cutoff and code potential, access, recognition, prospective value, uncertainty, authority, and any contemporaneous pursuit. Realization is **not yet observable** even if the coder knows the historical outcome.

### Pass B — Full trajectory

Coders receive later pursuit, realization, foreclosure, and retrospective evidence. They recode the later stages and may revise earlier stages only with a stated evidentiary reason.

## Reporting

Each completed episode should publish:

- the frozen Pass A source list;
- stage codes and exact supporting records;
- excluded outcome-bearing materials;
- independent coder disagreement;
- revisions after Pass B;
- alternative interpretations;
- and a bounded conclusion.

A successful coding exercise validates the instrument only provisionally. It does not validate Proposition 4, estimate an effect, or establish a general theory of recognition.
