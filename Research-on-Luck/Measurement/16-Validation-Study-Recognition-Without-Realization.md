# Validation Study 1 — Recognition without Realization

## Status

**Protocol Version 0.2 — Construct-validation pilot; prospective after FLEM-01**

This document specifies the first bounded validation study arising from the [Proposition-to-Evidence Map](../References/01-Proposition-to-Evidence-Map.md). It concerns Proposition 4: opportunity recognition is uneven.

The study does not assume that recognition causes realization. Its first task is to determine whether recognition and adjacent opportunity stages can be identified consistently without relying on realized outcomes.

## Version 0.2 Revision

Version 0.2 incorporates the completed [FLEM-01 methodological synthesis](18-FLEM-01-Methodological-Synthesis-and-Stage-2-Recommendation.md). It applies prospectively and does not revise the frozen FLEM-01 packets or coder records.

The revision:

- retains two independent coders for benchmark episodes;
- replaces routine independent packet audits with coordinator preflight and conditional escalation;
- uses only the number of outcome checkpoints required by the episode;
- standardizes component-status, realization-threshold, confidence, and contamination rules;
- limits adjudication to substantive disagreements;
- and introduces risk-tiered review for later replication and within-domain studies.

## Research Question

> Can potential, access, recognition, pursuit, realization, and foreclosure be distinguished using contemporaneous or near-contemporaneous evidence across cases with different outcomes?

Secondary questions are:

- Can false or premature recognition be distinguished from valid recognition?
- Can recognition be separated from authority, resources, pursuit, and implementation?
- At what system level does recognition occur?
- How much does coding change when outcome information is withheld?
- Which evidence is necessary before recognition can be inferred?

## Propositional Target

[General Theory Proposition 4](../General%20Theory/01-Core-Propositions.md) states that systems exposed to similar conditions may differ in what they perceive and interpret as actionable.

The current evidence map classifies this as a retained hypothesis and test priority. It does not yet establish:

- how common uneven recognition is;
- whether recognition has an independent effect on realization;
- whether one recognition construct transfers across domains;
- or whether later success biases retrospective coding.

## Relationship to Existing Architecture

The protocol adopts the opportunity stages specified in [Formalization 12](../Formalization/12-Opportunity-Dynamics.md) and the indicators developed in [Measurement 12](12-Opportunity-and-Constraint-Indicators.md).

It also follows the identification and validation requirements in [Measurement 15](15-Identification-Validation-and-Measurement-Summary.md).

## Unit of Analysis

The unit is an **opportunity episode**:

> a bounded relationship between a specified focal system and a candidate possibility during a stated time interval.

Each episode must specify:

- focal system;
- relevant environment;
- level of analysis;
- candidate opportunity;
- opening and closing dates;
- contemporary information set;
- access conditions;
- decision authority;
- resources and constraints;
- actions taken;
- and the outcome horizon.

An individual’s perception should not automatically be coded as organizational recognition. Nested levels must be recorded separately.

## Stage Definitions

| Stage | Operational question | Minimum evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Potential | Was a consequential action or development plausibly possible? | Contemporary technical, institutional, or market conditions supporting feasibility |
| Access | Could the focal system encounter or act on the possibility? | Information channels, legal permission, resources, position, or institutional access |
| Recognition | Did an authorized or relevant actor interpret the possibility as actionable? | Dated statement, proposal, experiment, memorandum, allocation request, or equivalent record |
| Pursuit | Did the system commit action or resources? | Budget, staffing, experiment, project, agreement, product, policy, or implementation record |
| Realization | Did the pursued relationship produce the specified outcome by the chosen horizon? | Predefined outcome evidence, not retrospective success language |
| Foreclosure | Did the opportunity become infeasible for the focal system? | Loss of access, expiry, competing standard, institutional block, resource exhaustion, or changed environment |

## Recognition Categories

Each episode receives one primary category:

1. **No demonstrated access**
2. **Access without demonstrated recognition**
3. **Contested recognition**
4. **Recognition without pursuit**
5. **Recognition followed by delayed or partial pursuit**
6. **Recognition followed by pursuit without realization**
7. **Recognition followed by realization**
8. **Premature or false recognition**
9. **Indeterminate**

The indeterminate category is substantive. Missing evidence must not be converted automatically into nonrecognition.

## Pilot Cases

The construct-validation pilot uses five existing cases selected for outcome and stage variation.

| Case | Expected coding challenge | Pilot role |
|---|---|---|
| [Fleming and Penicillin](../Empirical%20Studies/Science/01-Alexander-Fleming-and-Penicillin.md) | Separating initial observation, interpretation, later development, and institutional realization | Recognition followed by delayed realization |
| [Cold Fusion](../Empirical%20Studies/Science/11-Cold-Fusion.md) | Distinguishing recognition from premature interpretation and independent validation | False or premature recognition |
| [Betamax](../Empirical%20Studies/Technology/10-Betamax.md) | Separating recognition of a broad opportunity from recognition of selection conditions | Pursuit without dominant realization |
| [Microsoft](../Empirical%20Studies/Business/04-Microsoft.md) | Coding multiple episodes within one organization across different outcomes | Within-system recognition variation |
| [Kodak](../Empirical%20Studies/Business/06-Kodak.md) | Separating technical awareness, organizational recognition, divided pursuit, and foreclosure | Recognition with partial pursuit and unrealized paths |

These cases are purposive and heterogeneous. They test construct portability and expose ambiguity. They cannot estimate prevalence, effect size, or cross-domain generality.

### Pilot Progress

- **FLEM-01 — completed and methodologically closed.** The episode provisionally demonstrated separability of recognition, pursuit, realization, and actor attribution.
- **Cold Fusion — next controlled replication, subject to feasibility and packet preflight.** It will test premature or false recognition and corrective replication within Science.
- **Betamax, Microsoft, and Kodak — retained but gated.** Their necessity and ordering will be reconsidered after Cold Fusion rather than assumed in advance.

## Episode Construction

The pilot should decompose each case into one or more episodes rather than assigning one label to the entire case.

For every episode, create a record with:

| Field | Description |
|---|---|
| Episode ID | Stable identifier |
| Focal system | Individual, team, organization, network, or institution |
| Nested level | Relevant component or higher-level system |
| Candidate opportunity | Precisely stated possibility |
| Time window | Opening, decision, and closing dates |
| Contemporary information | What relevant actors could have known |
| Potential evidence | Evidence of feasibility |
| Access evidence | Evidence of access |
| Recognition evidence | Dated interpretive evidence |
| Pursuit evidence | Action and resource commitment |
| Realization criterion | Outcome defined before coding realization |
| Foreclosure evidence | When and why feasibility ended |
| Alternative interpretation | Strongest competing coding |
| Evidence quality | Primary, secondary, contextual, or missing |
| Confidence | High, medium, low, or indeterminate |

## Evidence Hierarchy

Preferred evidence, in descending order:

1. contemporaneous primary records;
2. dated technical, organizational, or institutional documents;
3. near-contemporaneous testimony with corroboration;
4. scholarly historical reconstruction;
5. retrospective memoir or interview;
6. later popular narrative.

Later success narratives should not establish earlier recognition without independent support.

## Blinded Coding Design

To test hindsight sensitivity, coding should occur in two passes.

### Pass A — Pre-outcome Packet

Coders receive evidence only through the episode’s decision point. They code potential, access, recognition, perceived value, uncertainty, and expected feasibility.

### Pass B — Full-Trajectory Packet

Coders receive subsequent pursuit, realization, foreclosure, and outcome evidence. They may revise stage boundaries but must record every revision.

The difference between Pass A and Pass B is a direct indicator of hindsight-sensitive classification.

## Coding Procedure

1. Define the episode, focal system, candidate, cutoff, realization criterion, horizon, and required checkpoint structure.
2. Assemble and log the pre-outcome evidence before interpreting the full trajectory.
3. Complete the Version 0.2 preflight checklist for boundary stability, source sufficiency, outcome leakage, actor attribution, component definitions, and link integrity.
4. Have the coordinator freeze the packet when no independent-audit trigger is active.
5. Have two coders classify Pass A independently and lock both records before comparison or later release.
6. Release the minimum full-trajectory packet or sequential checkpoints required by the research question.
7. Lock later codes and every recognition revision with the new evidence and reason.
8. Generate a fixed-matrix comparison of stage codes, categories, actor boundaries, component statuses, and confidence fields.
9. Adjudicate only substantive disagreements under pre-registered rules and preserve indeterminate cases.
10. Escalate to independent audit only when a material trigger applies; otherwise publish the agreement record and methodological limitations.

## Review Architecture

| Tier | Use | Review requirement |
|---|---|---|
| Tier 1 — Full validation | New instrument or materially ambiguous episode | Two independent coders plus independent packet audit |
| Tier 2 — Controlled replication | Subsequent episode using Version 0.2 | Coordinator preflight and freeze, two independent coders, disagreement-only adjudication, conditional audit |
| Tier 3 — Larger empirical study | Within-domain episode population | Double-coded reliability subsample, exception review, and aggregate validity tests |

An independent audit is triggered by a post-collection candidate change, contestable realization criterion, material actor-boundary dispute, central retrospective inference, unresolved outcome leakage, unstable conjunctive components, substantive unauthorized exposure, material coder disagreement, unresolved source conflict, or proposed promotion of a major theoretical claim.

Minor wording or confidence differences do not trigger review unless they alter a substantive conclusion.

## Reliability and Validity Tests

### Inter-coder Reliability

Report agreement for:

- access;
- recognition;
- pursuit;
- realization;
- foreclosure;
- recognition category;
- and system level.

A numerical coefficient may be used if the number of episodes becomes adequate, but raw disagreement tables remain necessary in a small pilot.

### Construct Validity

Recognition coding should correlate with contemporaneous interpretive evidence, not merely later resource commitment or success.

### Discriminant Validity

Recognition must remain distinguishable from:

- exposure;
- access;
- technical capability;
- decision authority;
- pursuit;
- optimism;
- and realization.

### Hindsight Sensitivity

Measure how often recognition classifications change after outcome evidence is disclosed.

### Cross-Case Portability

Identify which fields retain meaning across Science, Technology, and Business and which require domain-specific indicators.

## Competing Explanations

A recognition–realization association could arise because:

- systems with greater resources both document recognition and pursue more;
- powerful actors receive more archival attention;
- successful episodes preserve more records;
- recognition is coded retrospectively;
- decision authority, not perception, drives pursuit;
- or cases were selected to fit the framework.

The pilot should expose these problems rather than claim to solve all of them.

## Decision Rules

### Advance to Stage 2 if:

- recognition can be coded separately from access, pursuit, and realization;
- coder disagreement is explainable and reducible through rules;
- outcome disclosure does not systematically reverse recognition coding;
- and at least one domain offers sufficient comparable episodes and primary evidence.

### Revise the Construct if:

- recognition cannot be separated from pursuit or authority;
- system-level coding remains unstable;
- false recognition cannot be distinguished from valid recognition ex ante;
- or domain-specific meanings overwhelm the shared definition.

### Stop or Reclassify if:

- recognition is consistently inferred only from outcome knowledge;
- available evidence cannot recover contemporary information sets;
- or the category adds no explanatory discrimination beyond access and pursuit.

## Stage 2 Design

If the pilot succeeds, Stage 2 should use a within-domain episode population rather than another heterogeneous case collection.

The preferred design should:

- define a sampling frame before outcome selection;
- include realized, unrealized, rejected, delayed, and false-positive episodes;
- use comparable evidence windows;
- code recognition without outcome information;
- measure authority, resources, and capability independently;
- and estimate whether recognition predicts pursuit or realization after competing explanations are considered.

The domain should be chosen according to evidence availability, not prominence.

## Outputs

The pilot should produce:

1. an episode-level coding template;
2. an evidence packet for each episode;
3. independent coder records;
4. a disagreement and adjudication log;
5. a construct-validity assessment;
6. a hindsight-sensitivity report;
7. and a Stage 2 recommendation.

## Limitations

This protocol does not itself supply contemporaneous sources or execute coding. The existing case narratives are starting points, not evidence packets. External source collection remains necessary.

The five pilot cases are not independent or representative. A successful pilot would validate a coding approach provisionally, not Proposition 4’s causal or cross-domain generality.

## Current Conclusion

FLEM-01 provides provisional evidence that recognition can be separated from access, pursuit, realization, and later collective attribution in one delayed-realization episode. It does not establish portability, prevalence, causal effect, or the validity of Proposition 4.

The next step is a Tier 2 Cold Fusion episode testing premature or false recognition and corrective replication. Advancement to Stage 2 remains gated until that contrasting episode shows whether Version 0.2 reduces ambiguity without reproducing FLEM-01’s full audit burden.
