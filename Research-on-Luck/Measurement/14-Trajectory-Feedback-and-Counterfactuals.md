# Trajectory, Feedback, and Counterfactual Measurement

## Purpose

This document operationalizes trajectories, feedback, amplification, attenuation, path dependence, resilience, regime change, and counterfactual alternatives.

The central principle is that a terminal outcome does not reveal the process that produced it.

---

## 1. Trajectory Unit

A trajectory record should specify:

- focal system;
- state variables;
- baseline;
- ordered observation times;
- exposures and interactions;
- responses;
- intermediate states;
- terminal horizon;
- and missing intervals.

Where observation is irregular, event time or period coding may be preferable to artificial equal intervals.

---

## 2. Divergence and Convergence

Trajectory divergence requires a comparison:

- between systems;
- against a prior trend;
- against a matched or synthetic comparison;
- or across feasible branches.

The choice of baseline affects the claim.

Convergence in one outcome may conceal divergence in resources, resilience, or future opportunity.

---

## 3. Feedback Evidence

A feedback claim requires evidence that an earlier consequence changed later conditions affecting the same or connected process.

Useful evidence includes:

- resource reinvestment;
- changed expectations;
- expanded access;
- institutional response;
- network growth;
- altered selection;
- or countervailing intervention.

Repeated association is not enough if the loop is not demonstrated.

---

## 4. Amplification and Attenuation

Measurement should define:

- initial difference;
- variable affected;
- comparison group or baseline;
- time window;
- and mechanism.

Amplification occurs when a difference grows through a specified process. Attenuation occurs when it shrinks.

Absolute growth by all systems is not necessarily amplification of differences.

---

## 5. Path Dependence

Evidence for path dependence should establish:

1. an early state or branch;
2. multiple feasible alternatives at that time;
3. a carrying mechanism;
4. changed later feasibility or transition;
5. persistence beyond the initial event.

Possible carrying mechanisms include sunk costs, network effects, learning, standards, institutions, coordination, and power.

Historical sequence alone is insufficient.

---

## 6. Lock-In

Lock-in indicators may include:

- switching costs;
- complementary investments;
- standard dependence;
- legal commitments;
- network externalities;
- political vetoes;
- and disappearance of alternatives.

The existence of persistence does not prove inefficiency or permanence.

---

## 7. Resilience

Specify which form is measured:

- resistance;
- recovery;
- adaptation;
- or transformation.

Record:

- disturbance;
- function or identity preserved;
- depth of loss;
- recovery time;
- distribution of costs;
- and later vulnerability.

Aggregate recovery may coexist with irreversible losses among components.

---

## 8. Regime Change

A regime-change claim requires evidence that transition rules, governing parameters, boundaries, or feasible states changed.

Indicators may include:

- new institutional rules;
- altered feedback structure;
- changed network topology;
- new technological standard;
- redefined system boundary;
- or discontinuous transition behavior.

A large state change without altered future rules is not necessarily regime change.

---

## 9. Turning Points

A turning point should be identified by evidence that a transition changed the later path, not merely because it appears dramatic in retrospect.

Useful designs include:

- process tracing;
- interrupted time series;
- threshold analysis;
- sequence comparison;
- and contemporaneous branch evidence.

Uncertainty about timing should be reported.

---

## 10. Counterfactuals

Counterfactual evidence may come from:

- randomized assignment;
- natural experiments;
- matched comparisons;
- synthetic controls;
- interrupted trends;
- comparable cases;
- contemporaneous plans;
- near misses;
- and mechanism-based historical reconstruction.

Each method relies on assumptions. Those assumptions should be stated and stress-tested.

---

## 11. Feasible Alternative Set

Counterfactuals should be limited to alternatives defensibly feasible from the ex ante state.

Evidence of feasibility includes:

- capability;
- access;
- resources;
- institutional permission;
- timing;
- and analogous realization elsewhere.

The more unsupported changes required, the weaker the counterfactual.

---

## 12. Unrealized and Missing Paths

Unrealized paths often leave sparse evidence.

Sources may include:

- rejected proposals;
- abandoned experiments;
- alternative designs;
- minority reports;
- failed organizations;
- unpublished work;
- and contemporaneous forecasts.

Absence from surviving archives should not be treated as evidence that no alternative existed.

---

## 13. Selection and Survivorship

Trajectory samples should document entry, attrition, survival, and visibility.

Where possible, include:

- failures;
- delayed cases;
- suppressed alternatives;
- organizations that exited;
- discoveries not recognized;
- and policies not adopted.

Survivorship adjustment is essential when the outcome affects inclusion in the dataset.

---

## 14. Multiple Horizons

Evaluation should be repeated at plausible horizons.

An initially favorable trajectory may create fragility; an adverse event may induce learning; an intervention may shift costs across levels.

The horizon should be chosen before interpreting valence where possible.

---

## 15. Confidence in Trajectory Claims

Report separately:

- confidence in state measurement;
- confidence in sequence;
- confidence in mechanism;
- confidence in comparison;
- and confidence in counterfactual.

A strong timeline with a weak counterfactual should not be presented as strong causal identification.

---

## Conclusion

Trajectory measurement preserves sequence, feedback, and feasible alternatives.

It requires more than endpoint comparison. By identifying carrying mechanisms, selection, thresholds, and changing transition structures, it creates a disciplined basis for studying how uncertain differences persist, dissipate, reverse, or transform.
