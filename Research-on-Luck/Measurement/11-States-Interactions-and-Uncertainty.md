# Measuring States, Interactions, and Uncertainty

## Purpose

This document operationalizes the state-transition core of the formal architecture.

Measurement should distinguish system state, environmental state, interaction, exposure, and uncertainty rather than collapsing them into an outcome or residual.

---

## 1. Focal System and Boundary

Every study should record:

- focal system;
- level of analysis;
- analytical boundary;
- internal components;
- relevant environment;
- time horizon;
- and evidence supporting the boundary.

Boundary choices affect whether a factor is coded as capability, environment, interaction, or external shock.

Nested systems require separate identifiers for component, organization, institution, and larger system where relevant.

---

## 2. System State

A system state may include indicators of:

- capabilities;
- resources;
- information;
- network position;
- rules and routines;
- authority;
- structure;
- and prior trajectory.

A baseline should be measured before the focal exposure where possible.

Historical studies should distinguish contemporaneous evidence from later reconstruction.

---

## 3. Environmental State

Relevant environmental indicators may include:

- institutions and laws;
- other systems;
- market or political conditions;
- knowledge and technology;
- physical conditions;
- resource distributions;
- and historical regime.

The relevant environment is system-specific. Aggregate conditions may not represent effective exposure for every system.

---

## 4. Interaction and Exposure

An environmental event is not identical to exposure.

Measurement should record:

- event;
- channel;
- timing;
- intensity;
- duration;
- proximity;
- mediation;
- and whether the system perceived the interaction.

Exposure evidence may come from records, location, network ties, transactions, communications, participation, institutional eligibility, or direct observation.

A binary exposed/not-exposed variable may be insufficient when timing, dose, or channel matters.

---

## 5. Action and Non-Action

Response measures should include both action and non-action.

Possible indicators include:

- decision records;
- resource commitments;
- delay;
- experimentation;
- search;
- avoidance;
- reorganization;
- and policy or strategy change.

Non-action should be coded by mechanism where evidence allows: non-recognition, deliberate waiting, constraint, competing objective, prohibition, or incapacity.

---

## 6. Transition Measurement

A transition record should include:

1. prior state;
2. environmental condition;
3. interaction or exposure;
4. system response;
5. immediate state change;
6. intermediate change;
7. later feedback;
8. terminal observation horizon.

This structure supports sequence and process analysis.

A before-and-after difference alone cannot identify which part of the transition produced change.

---

## 7. Trajectory Records

A trajectory dataset should preserve ordered states rather than only endpoints.

Useful forms include:

- event histories;
- panel observations;
- sequences;
- timelines;
- network snapshots;
- institutional-period coding;
- and qualitative process records.

Irregular observation intervals and missing transitions should be documented.

---

## 8. Uncertainty Types

Measurement should distinguish:

### Stochastic uncertainty

Evidence supports probabilistic variation in a specified process.

### Epistemic uncertainty

The focal system or observer lacked relevant knowledge.

### Model uncertainty

Several plausible models imply different interpretations.

### Ambiguity

Possible outcomes were partly recognized but probabilities were unclear.

### Novelty

Relevant possibilities were absent from prior categories.

### Strategic uncertainty

Consequences depended on other adaptive systems.

### Dynamic uncertainty

Feedback and environmental change altered later possibilities.

These types may overlap. Coding should identify whose uncertainty, at what time, and with which information.

---

## 9. Ex Ante Information Set

A credible ex ante reconstruction should use evidence available before the outcome:

- forecasts;
- plans;
- correspondence;
- meeting records;
- published knowledge;
- market signals;
- rules;
- and documented expectations.

Later accounts should be treated as ex post evidence and assessed for hindsight bias.

---

## 10. Risk and Probability

Where probabilities are available, measurement should record:

- model used;
- reference class;
- data window;
- calibration;
- uncertainty around estimates;
- and whether the focal system knew or used them.

Estimated probability does not measure luck. It characterizes one part of the ex ante uncertainty structure.

---

## 11. Measurement Error and Process Uncertainty

Measurement error concerns the observation process. Process uncertainty concerns the system or environment.

They should be reported separately.

Missing variables, poor model fit, and unexplained residuals should not be recoded as luck without evidence of uncertain interaction and consequence.

---

## 12. Cross-Level Transitions

A transition at one level may be exposure at another.

Examples include:

- organizational decision affecting individuals;
- institutional reform affecting organizations;
- aggregate adoption changing individual opportunity;
- individual innovation changing an ecosystem.

Cross-level claims need data connecting the levels rather than parallel trends alone.

---

## 13. Evidence Strength

For each state or transition measure, classify evidence as:

- directly observed;
- reconstructed from contemporaneous evidence;
- inferred from multiple indicators;
- retrospectively reported;
- or unknown.

Sensitivity analysis should test how conclusions change under plausible alternative coding.

---

## Conclusion

States, environments, interactions, and uncertainty require separate measurement.

Preserving baseline, exposure channel, response, sequence, information position, and level of analysis makes trajectory claims more defensible and prevents luck from becoming a label for incomplete data.
