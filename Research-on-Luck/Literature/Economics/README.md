# Economics

## Overview

The **Economics** directory reviews how economics has investigated the role of luck in shaping individual and collective outcomes.

Unlike philosophy, which primarily examines the conceptual nature of luck, economics focuses on how luck influences observable phenomena such as income, wealth, labor markets, education, entrepreneurship, innovation, and economic mobility.

The objective of this directory is not to review economic theory in general, but to understand how economists have conceptualized, measured, and analyzed the effects of luck, and how those contributions inform the broader **Research on Luck** project.

---

# Objectives

The primary objectives of this directory are to:

* Review economic theories concerning luck and skill.
* Examine the role of luck in economic outcomes.
* Understand how economists distinguish luck from effort and ability.
* Evaluate empirical methods used to identify the effects of luck.
* Examine how institutions influence the distribution and consequences of luck.
* Identify opportunities for interdisciplinary integration.

---

# Scope

Topics appropriate for this directory include:

* Luck versus skill
* Income and wealth inequality
* Social mobility
* Labor markets
* Human capital
* Opportunity
* Entrepreneurship
* Innovation
* Institutions
* Incentives
* Economic development
* Intergenerational mobility

The emphasis is on understanding the economic consequences of luck rather than developing new economic theories.

# Review Methodology

Each review addresses a common set of questions.

* What economic problem is being studied?
* How is luck defined or treated?
* What theoretical framework is employed?
* What empirical evidence is available?
* What are the principal contributions?
* What are the limitations?
* What implications arise for the **Research on Luck** project?

Using a consistent methodology facilitates comparison across disciplines.

---

# Relationship to the Research on Luck Project

Economics contributes a distinctive perspective to the study of luck.

Rather than asking **what luck is**, economics primarily asks:

* How much do outcomes depend upon luck?
* How should luck be distinguished from skill?
* How do institutions influence opportunities?
* How does luck contribute to inequality?
* How should societies respond to unequal outcomes?

These questions complement the conceptual analyses developed in philosophy while introducing empirical methods and quantitative models that become increasingly important for subsequent stages of the project.

---

# Guiding Principles

The Economics literature should be reviewed according to the following principles.

* Distinguish conceptual assumptions from empirical findings.
* Separate positive analysis from normative arguments.
* Distinguish statistical residuals from luck whenever possible.
* Evaluate both theoretical and empirical contributions.
* Recognize that different economic models may adopt different assumptions concerning uncertainty, information, and human behavior.
* Compare economic perspectives with those of other disciplines rather than considering them in isolation.

---

# Long-Term Goal

The long-term goal of the **Economics** directory is to understand how economic research explains the influence of luck on human outcomes while identifying both its strengths and its limitations.

The broader **Research on Luck** project seeks to build upon these contributions by integrating economic theory with philosophy, psychology, statistics, complexity science, and other disciplines to develop a more comprehensive understanding of luck as a multidimensional phenomenon.
