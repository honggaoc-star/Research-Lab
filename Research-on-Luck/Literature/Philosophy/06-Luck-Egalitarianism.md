# Luck Egalitarianism

## Abstract

Luck egalitarianism is one of the most influential theories in contemporary political philosophy. It argues that inequalities arising from circumstances beyond individual control deserve different moral treatment from inequalities resulting from deliberate choices.

This review examines the principal ideas underlying luck egalitarianism, its major contributions, and the criticisms it has received. While luck egalitarianism offers an important normative framework for evaluating fairness and equality, it addresses only one aspect of the broader phenomenon of luck.

The review considers its relevance to the **Research on Luck** project and its implications for future interdisciplinary research.

---

# 1. Introduction

Few philosophical theories have connected luck with public policy as directly as luck egalitarianism.

The central question is straightforward.

> **Should society compensate individuals for disadvantages resulting from luck?**

The answer depends upon how one distinguishes between circumstances beyond individual control and consequences arising from deliberate choice.

Luck egalitarianism attempts to provide such a distinction.

---

# 2. The Central Idea

Luck egalitarianism begins with a simple moral intuition.

Individuals should not be disadvantaged because of circumstances they did not choose.

Examples include:

* Birthplace
* Family background
* Natural abilities
* Disability
* Historical circumstances

Conversely,

inequalities resulting from voluntary choices may be regarded as morally acceptable.

This distinction has become one of the defining characteristics of the literature.

---

# 3. Brute Luck and Option Luck

Many discussions distinguish between two forms of luck.

## Brute Luck

Brute luck refers to outcomes arising from circumstances beyond an individual's control.

Examples include:

* Genetic inheritance
* Natural disasters
* Childhood environment

---

## Option Luck

Option luck concerns outcomes resulting from voluntary choices involving known risks.

Examples include:

* Investments
* Gambling
* Entrepreneurial decisions

This distinction has become one of the most influential concepts within political philosophy.

---

# 4. Contributions

Luck egalitarianism contributes several important ideas.

First,

it distinguishes different sources of inequality.

Second,

it emphasizes the role of individual responsibility.

Third,

it links theories of luck with institutional design.

Fourth,

it provides a framework for evaluating equality of opportunity rather than equality of outcomes.

These contributions extend well beyond philosophy into economics and public policy.

---

# 5. Criticisms

The literature has identified several important criticisms.

Some argue that the distinction between brute luck and option luck is often unclear.

Many decisions involve circumstances that individuals neither fully control nor fully understand.

Others argue that emphasizing personal responsibility may overlook structural and institutional constraints.

Additional critics question whether governments can reliably distinguish voluntary choices from unavoidable circumstances.

These debates remain active.

---

# 6. Limitations

From the perspective of the **Research on Luck** project, luck egalitarianism also exhibits limitations.

The literature primarily addresses normative questions.

Its principal concern is how societies ought to respond to unequal outcomes.

Less attention is given to:

* how luck originates,
* how luck propagates,
* how luck interacts with dynamic systems,
* how luck can be measured,
* or how different mechanisms of luck operate.

Consequently, luck egalitarianism should be viewed as an important ethical framework rather than a general scientific theory of luck.

---

# 7. Implications for Research on Luck

Luck egalitarianism raises questions that extend beyond political philosophy.

If luck can be distinguished from deliberate choice,

then understanding the mechanisms of luck becomes increasingly important.

Scientific models capable of identifying different sources of luck may eventually inform discussions concerning:

* equality of opportunity,
* institutional design,
* education,
* insurance,
* taxation,
* and social mobility.

Rather than providing policy recommendations, the **Research on Luck** project seeks to develop conceptual and empirical tools capable of supporting such discussions.

---

# 8. Open Questions

Several important questions remain unresolved.

* Can brute luck and option luck be distinguished empirically?
* Do institutions alter the distribution of luck?
* Can educational systems reduce the influence of brute luck?
* Is equality of opportunity measurable?
* How should cumulative luck be treated within theories of justice?

These questions naturally connect philosophy with economics, sociology, and political science.

---

# 9. Conclusion

Luck egalitarianism represents one of the most influential attempts to connect philosophical theories of luck with questions of justice and public policy.

Its central contribution lies in distinguishing between inequalities arising from uncontrollable circumstances and those associated with deliberate choices.

For the **Research on Luck** project, this distinction provides valuable normative insights while also highlighting the need for more comprehensive conceptual, theoretical, and empirical models capable of explaining how luck actually operates.

Luck egalitarianism therefore represents an important philosophical perspective on the ethical implications of luck rather than a complete theory of the phenomenon.
