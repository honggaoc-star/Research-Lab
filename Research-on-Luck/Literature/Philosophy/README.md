# Philosophy

## Overview

The **Philosophy** directory reviews philosophical perspectives on luck and their relevance to the broader **Research on Luck** project.

Among all academic disciplines, philosophy has examined the concept of luck most directly. Questions concerning chance, fortune, responsibility, free will, knowledge, and justice have been debated for centuries and continue to influence contemporary discussions in ethics, epistemology, metaphysics, and political philosophy.

The objective of this directory is not to advocate a particular philosophical position, but to understand how philosophical theories define, analyze, and evaluate luck, and how those insights contribute to a broader scientific framework.

---

# Objectives

The primary objectives of this directory are to:

* Survey major philosophical theories of luck.
* Clarify fundamental philosophical concepts.
* Compare competing definitions and interpretations.
* Identify areas of agreement and disagreement.
* Evaluate the relevance of philosophical theories to scientific research on luck.
* Identify opportunities for interdisciplinary integration.

---

# Scope

Topics appropriate for this directory include:

* General philosophy of luck
* Moral luck
* Epistemic luck
* Metaphysical perspectives on luck
* Free will and determinism
* Responsibility and agency
* Chance and coincidence
* Fortune and contingency
* Justice and equality of opportunity

The emphasis is conceptual rather than mathematical or empirical.

# Review Methodology

Each literature review should address:

* The central philosophical question.
* Major schools of thought.
* Influential contributors.
* Principal arguments.
* Criticisms and limitations.
* Relevance to the Research on Luck project.

The emphasis is critical evaluation rather than exhaustive historical coverage.

---

# Relationship to the Research on Luck Project

Philosophy provides many of the conceptual foundations for studying luck, including questions about:

* the nature of luck,
* human agency,
* responsibility,
* chance,
* and moral evaluation.

However, the **Research on Luck** project extends beyond philosophy by integrating perspectives from economics, psychology, statistics, complexity science, and other disciplines.

The philosophical literature therefore serves as one important foundation rather than the complete theoretical framework.

---

# Long-Term Goal

The long-term goal of this directory is to establish a comprehensive understanding of how philosophy has approached the concept of luck, while identifying conceptual insights that remain valuable for contemporary interdisciplinary research.

Where philosophical theories leave unresolved questions, those questions become opportunities for further theoretical development within the broader Research on Luck project.
