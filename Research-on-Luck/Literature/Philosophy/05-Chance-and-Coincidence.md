# Chance and Coincidence

## Abstract

Chance and coincidence are among the concepts most frequently associated with luck. In everyday language, the three terms are often used interchangeably. Philosophical analysis, however, has generally treated them as distinct concepts describing different aspects of uncertain events and human experience.

This review examines how philosophers have understood chance and coincidence, how these concepts relate to luck, and what their distinctions contribute to a broader scientific understanding of luck. While chance and coincidence explain important characteristics of uncertain events, neither alone provides a complete account of luck.

---

# 1. Introduction

People frequently describe unexpected events as occurring "by chance" or "by coincidence."

Examples include:

* Meeting an old friend unexpectedly.
* Discovering an important scientific result accidentally.
* Arriving just in time for an opportunity.
* Escaping danger by a narrow margin.

Such events are often described as lucky.

Yet philosophy has long questioned whether chance, coincidence, and luck actually refer to the same phenomenon.

Understanding these distinctions is essential for developing a rigorous conceptual framework.

---

# 2. Chance

The concept of chance generally refers to the occurrence of events that are not predictable with certainty or that lack intentional design.

Different philosophical traditions interpret chance differently.

Some regard chance as an objective feature of reality.

Others view chance as reflecting limitations in human knowledge.

Regardless of interpretation, chance primarily concerns **how events occur** rather than **how those events affect individuals**.

---

# 3. Coincidence

Coincidence refers to the unexpected conjunction of events that appear meaningfully related despite lacking an obvious causal connection.

Examples include:

* Two strangers discovering they were born on the same day.
* Independently developing the same scientific idea.
* Unexpectedly encountering someone immediately after discussing them.

Coincidence therefore concerns patterns perceived by observers rather than isolated events.

Whether coincidences possess deeper significance remains a matter of philosophical debate.

---

# 4. Relationship to Luck

Although closely related, chance, coincidence, and luck emphasize different aspects of experience.

Chance concerns uncertainty in the occurrence of events.

Coincidence concerns unexpected relationships among events.

Luck concerns the influence of such events on meaningful outcomes.

Consequently,

chance and coincidence may exist without luck,

and luck may arise without remarkable coincidence.

The concepts overlap but are not identical.

---

# 5. Contributions to the Study of Luck

The philosophical literature contributes several important insights.

First,

unexpectedness alone does not constitute luck.

Second,

coincidence depends partly upon human interpretation.

Third,

chance and coincidence may create conditions under which luck emerges.

Finally,

meaning and consequence play important roles in distinguishing luck from merely unusual events.

These observations reinforce the importance of separating event generation from outcome evaluation.

---

# 6. Limitations

From the perspective of the **Research on Luck** project, the literature also exhibits limitations.

Most discussions remain conceptual.

Little attention is given to:

* probability theory,
* statistical coincidence,
* complex systems,
* network effects,
* institutional processes,
* or empirical investigation.

Connections between chance, coincidence, and dynamic social processes remain largely unexplored.

---

# 7. Implications for Research on Luck

The present project benefits from distinguishing these concepts rather than treating them as interchangeable.

Chance describes one way uncertain events occur.

Coincidence describes how observers recognize unexpected relationships among events.

Luck concerns how uncertain circumstances influence realized outcomes.

This distinction supports the broader conceptual framework developed in the Foundations section, where luck is treated as a relational concept rather than simply another name for randomness or chance.

---

# 8. Open Questions

The philosophical literature leaves several important questions unresolved.

* Can chance exist in a deterministic universe?
* Are coincidences objective or observer-dependent?
* Does coincidence require meaning, or merely improbability?
* Can luck exist without either chance or coincidence?
* How should chance, coincidence, and luck be represented within a unified scientific framework?

These questions remain important for future interdisciplinary research.

---

# 9. Conclusion

The philosophical study of chance and coincidence demonstrates that these concepts illuminate important aspects of uncertain events without fully explaining the phenomenon of luck.

Chance addresses the occurrence of events.

Coincidence addresses unexpected relationships among events.

Luck addresses the consequences of uncertain events for individuals, organizations, and societies.

Recognizing these distinctions provides a stronger conceptual foundation for the scientific study of luck and helps clarify one of the most common sources of ambiguity in both philosophical and everyday discussions.
