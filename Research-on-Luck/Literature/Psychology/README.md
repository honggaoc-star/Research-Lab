# Psychology

## Overview

Psychology investigates how individuals perceive, interpret, evaluate, and respond to uncertain events. While philosophy examines the conceptual nature of luck, economics investigates its influence on observable outcomes, and statistics provides methods for reasoning under uncertainty, psychology focuses on how people experience uncertainty and attribute outcomes to luck.

Importantly, psychology does **not** attempt to determine whether luck objectively exists. Instead, it investigates how people perceive luck, how beliefs about luck influence judgment and decision making, and how cognitive processes shape interpretations of uncertain events.

For the **Research on Luck** project, psychology provides the behavioral foundation for understanding why individuals often interpret similar events differently. Concepts such as attribution, agency, perceived control, superstition, optimism, cognitive bias, and decision making under uncertainty help explain how subjective experiences of luck emerge from interactions between uncertain events and human cognition.

Rather than asking *What is luck?*, *How does luck influence outcomes?*, or *How should uncertainty be represented?*, psychology asks:

> **How do people perceive, interpret, attribute, and respond to uncertain events?**

This perspective complements the conceptual foundations established in philosophy, the empirical investigations developed in economics, and the methodological framework provided by statistics.
