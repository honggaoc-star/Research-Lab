# Formalization Summary

## Purpose

This document integrates the additive Formalization series, files 10–14, and defines the minimum structure of a bounded formal model.

The objective is not a single universal equation of luck. It is a reusable way to specify systems, environments, interactions, uncertainty, relationships, mechanisms, and trajectories clearly enough for measurement and testing.

---

## 1. Formal Architecture

### Working primitives

- System
- Environment
- Interaction
- Uncertainty

### Derived conditions

- opportunity landscape;
- constraints;
- information;
- resources;
- variation.

### System capabilities

- perception;
- search;
- learning;
- coordination;
- adaptive capacity.

### Dynamic processes

- response;
- selection;
- adaptation;
- discovery;
- innovation;
- institutionalization.

### Long-term effects

- amplification and attenuation;
- path dependence;
- resilience;
- transformation;
- regime change.

### Evaluation

- realized opportunity;
- good or bad luck;
- missed or foreclosed opportunity;
- avoided harm;
- unexpected discovery.

Luck is an attribution across this architecture, not a primitive or unexplained term.

---

## 2. Relationship Types

The working relationship taxonomy is:

| Type | Formal role |
| --- | --- |
| Constitutive | Defines or composes a concept |
| Causal | Changes a later state or distribution |
| Enabling | Makes an interaction or transition feasible |
| Constraining | Restricts feasible interactions or transitions |
| Feedback | Makes consequences alter later conditions |

A relationship may occupy different roles in different bounded models. The role, level, horizon, mechanism, and boundary conditions must be stated.

---

## 3. Minimum Bounded Model

A model should identify:

1. focal system;
2. analytical boundary;
3. level of analysis;
4. environmental scope;
5. system and environmental states;
6. interaction structure;
7. time scale;
8. uncertainty structure;
9. feasible actions and transitions;
10. relationship types;
11. mechanisms;
12. feedback;
13. accessible and unrealized alternatives;
14. outcome horizon and evaluative standpoint;
15. observable implications;
16. and failure or revision conditions.

A model that omits these elements should explain why they are unnecessary for its question.

---

## 4. Formal Objects

Formal work should distinguish:

- **object:** entity or construct represented;
- **variable:** value that can differ;
- **state:** configuration at a time;
- **event:** temporally located change;
- **relationship:** connection among objects;
- **mechanism:** process producing change;
- **assumption:** condition provisionally accepted;
- **hypothesis:** relationship exposed to evaluation;
- **prediction:** observable implication;
- **evaluation:** judgment relative to a system, standard, and horizon.

This prevents definitions from being presented as predictions and correlations from being presented as mechanisms.

---

## 5. State and Transition Core

A general representation is:

[
X_i(t+1)=F(X_i(t),E_i(t),J_i(t),U_i(t)),
]

where:

- (X_i(t)) is the state of system (i);
- (E_i(t)) is its relevant environment;
- (J_i(t)) represents interaction;
- (U_i(t)) represents uncertainty;
- and (F) is a transition relationship.

The environment may also change endogenously:

[
E_i(t+1)=G(E_i(t),X_i(t),J_i(t),U_i(t)).
]

These expressions are scaffolds, not a claim that every domain shares the same functional form.

---

## 6. Opportunity Core

For system (i), the opportunity landscape (Omega_i(t)) is the structured set of relevant potential interactions and transitions.

Formalization should distinguish:

- potential;
- access;
- recognition;
- pursuit;
- realization;
- partial realization;
- foreclosure;
- and unrealized alternatives.

Counterfactuals should compare defensibly feasible paths rather than unconstrained imagined histories.

---

## 7. Adaptive Core

Capability affects perception, search, learning, coordination, and response but does not determine performance alone.

Observed outcome depends jointly on:

- capability;
- environment;
- interaction;
- uncertainty;
- constraints;
- and feedback.

Adaptation may change behavior, structure, relationships, or environment. It is not automatically beneficial.

Selection concerns differential persistence, growth, replication, or visibility. It does not establish superiority.

---

## 8. Recursive Core

Feedback connects consequences to later conditions.

Formal models should distinguish:

- reinforcement;
- countervailing adjustment;
- amplification;
- attenuation;
- stabilization;
- reversal;
- dissipation;
- threshold transition;
- lock-in;
- resilience;
- and regime change.

Path dependence requires a mechanism carrying relevant history forward. Regime change requires a change in transition structure, not merely a large state change.

---

## 9. Luck Sequence

### Ex ante — Luck Potential

The system faces uncertain exposures, opportunity relations, constraints, and feasible branches.

### Transition — Luck Realization

Interaction, recognition, action, feedback, and adaptation produce one trajectory while alternatives remain unrealized or become foreclosed.

### Ex post — Luck Attribution

The realized trajectory is evaluated relative to the ex ante state, feasible alternatives, and limits of prior intention, capability, and control.

Luck is not identical to stochasticity, a shock, a model residual, or an error term.

---

## 10. Formal Languages

Different questions may require:

- probability models;
- causal models;
- state-space representations;
- dynamic systems;
- network models;
- agent-based models;
- game-theoretic models;
- or counterfactual models.

No single language should be treated as sufficient for every domain. Mathematical complexity is warranted only when it clarifies assumptions, relationships, or implications.

---

## 11. Bridge to Measurement

Measurement should map each formal object to observable evidence.

Priority constructs include:

- state variables;
- boundary and level;
- environmental conditions;
- exposure;
- uncertainty;
- opportunity access;
- recognition;
- response;
- capability;
- feedback;
- trajectory;
- selection;
- and counterfactual feasibility.

Residual variation should not be labeled luck without evidence about uncertain interaction and consequence.

---

## 12. Evidence Status and General Theory

The new Formalization series supplies explicit structures and candidate hypotheses. It does not validate them.

A relationship may move toward General Theory only after:

1. conceptual clarity;
2. formal coherence;
3. defensible operationalization;
4. empirical support;
5. comparison across relevant domains;
6. identification of boundary conditions;
7. and serious consideration of alternatives.

Analogous language across cases is not sufficient.

---

## 13. Unresolved Formal Problems

Open issues include:

- how to represent novelty when possibilities are absent ex ante;
- how to identify opportunity landscapes empirically;
- how to model changing boundaries;
- how to separate stochasticity from missing mechanisms;
- how to compare nested levels;
- how to identify feedback and regime change;
- how to model power and institutional mediation;
- how to discipline unrealized counterfactuals;
- and whether the four primitives remain sufficient across domains.

---

## 14. Relationship to the Preserved Formalization

Files 01–07 preserve the project’s earlier mathematical and theoretical development.

Files 10–15 are additive. They reorganize later work around explicit relationships while leaving the original layer visible and intact.

Differences between the two layers document conceptual development and can guide later evaluation without requiring retrospective rewriting.

---

## Conclusion

Formalization turns the conceptual architecture into bounded relationship claims, transition structures, mechanisms, and observable implications.

Its product is not a universal theory of luck. It is a disciplined framework through which propositions can be measured, tested, compared, rejected, and—only when warranted—promoted toward general theory.
