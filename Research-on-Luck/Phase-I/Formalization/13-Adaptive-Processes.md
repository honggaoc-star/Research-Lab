# Adaptive Processes

## Purpose

This document formalizes perception, search, learning, coordination, adaptive capacity, response, adaptation, discovery, innovation, and selection.

These processes connect system capabilities to changing trajectories. Adaptation is possible but not necessarily beneficial, and realized performance is not a direct measure of capability.

---

## 1. Capability and Performance

Let \(K_i(t)\) denote the capability profile of system \(i\) at time \(t\).

Observed performance \(Y_i(t)\) may be represented as:

\[
Y_i(t)=p\big(K_i(t),E_i(t),J_i(t),U_i(t)\big),
\]

where \(E_i(t)\) is environment, \(J_i(t)\) is interaction, \(U_i(t)\) is uncertainty, and \(p\) is a performance relationship.

Because performance depends on all four terms, outcomes alone do not identify capability.

---

## 2. Perception

Let \(O_i(t)\) denote observable signals available to the system and \(M_i(t)\) its internal representation.

\[
M_i(t)=q\big(O_i(t),K_i(t),B_i(t),\eta_i(t)\big),
\]

where \(B_i(t)\) represents attention or interpretive bias, \(\eta_i(t)\) represents uncertainty, and \(q\) is a perception rule.

Perception can be incomplete, delayed, selective, or mistaken. The model should distinguish environmental condition, observable signal, and system interpretation.

---

## 3. Search

Let \(S_i(t)\) denote a search strategy over a feasible region \(\mathcal{F}_i(t)\).

Search may alter:

- information;
- exposure;
- opportunity recognition;
- resource use;
- and the environment itself.

A search policy may balance exploration and exploitation, but the framework does not assume that either is always optimal. Search costs, time windows, and institutional constraints should be explicit.

---

## 4. Learning

Let \(I_i(t)\) represent the system’s information or belief state.

\[
I_i(t+1)=L\big(I_i(t),O_i(t),A_i(t),Y_i(t)\big),
\]

where \(L\) is a learning rule, \(A_i(t)\) is action, and \(Y_i(t)\) is observed consequence.

Learning may improve a model, reinforce error, or overfit a selected history. Observed survival can bias learning if failed or unrealized alternatives are missing.

---

## 5. Coordination

For a system containing components or interacting with other systems, let \(C_i(t)\) denote coordination capacity.

Coordination affects whether information, resources, and actions can be combined. It may enable realization while constraining local autonomy.

Coordination should not be inferred from aggregate success; common exposure or centralized power may produce similar outcomes without effective coordination.

---

## 6. Adaptive Capacity

Adaptive capacity is the ability to change behavior, structure, capabilities, or relationships in response to actual or anticipated conditions.

A general representation is:

\[
K_i(t+1),A_i(t+1)=\Psi\big(X_i(t),E_i(t),M_i(t),Y_i(t)\big),
\]

where \(\Psi\) is an adaptation rule.

Adaptive capacity includes the possibility of response, not a guarantee of beneficial response.

It may be limited by resources, institutional rigidity, power, timing, commitments, or incompatible objectives.

---

## 7. Response and Non-Response

Let \(A_i(t)\) include both action and non-action.

Non-response may result from:

- non-recognition;
- deliberate waiting;
- insufficient resources;
- institutional prohibition;
- competing priorities;
- perceived downside;
- or inability to coordinate.

A model should not code all non-response as failure. Its ex ante rationale and constraints matter.

---

## 8. Adaptation

Adaptation is change induced by actual or anticipated conditions.

A useful evaluative distinction is:

- **behavioral adaptation** — changed action;
- **structural adaptation** — changed internal organization;
- **relational adaptation** — changed interaction patterns;
- **environmental modification** — changed external conditions.

Adaptation may improve fit under one regime while increasing fragility under another. Benefits require an explicit objective and horizon.

---

## 9. Discovery and Innovation

**Discovery** changes the system’s information about states, relationships, or possibilities.

**Innovation** changes practices, technologies, institutions, or interactions.

Both may be represented as transitions that expand or reorganize the feasible state space:

\[
\mathcal{F}_i(t+1)\neq\mathcal{F}_i(t).
\]

Neither is explained fully by novelty in the outcome. Models should represent exposure, prior knowledge, experimentation, recognition, resources, and diffusion.

---

## 10. Selection

Let \(W_i(t)\) represent persistence, replication, visibility, or growth under specified environmental conditions.

\[
W_i(t+1)=s\big(X_i(t),E_i(t),J_i(t),U_i(t)\big),
\]

where \(s\) is a selection relationship.

Selection may reflect capability, but also inherited position, timing, power, institutions, exposure, and environmental fit.

Persistence is therefore not proof of superiority. A model should specify what is selected, by which environment, at what level, and according to which criterion.

---

## 11. Systems Altering Environments

Adaptive action may change environmental state:

\[
E_i(t+1)=G\big(E_i(t),A_i(t),J_i(t)\big).
\]

Examples include institution building, technological change, market creation, network formation, and information production.

This creates endogenous feedback. A system can reshape later opportunities and the exposures of other systems.

---

## 12. Multiple Time Scales

Perception may be immediate, learning gradual, organizational adaptation slower, and institutional transformation slower still.

Models should state:

- the process clock;
- delay;
- memory;
- depreciation;
- and persistence.

Aggregating different speeds into one transition can obscure mechanisms.

---

## 13. Luck and Adaptive Processes

Luck potential concerns uncertain exposure and feasible trajectories. Luck realization occurs through perception, response, interaction, and feedback. Luck attribution evaluates the resulting trajectory.

Capability influences the process, but does not fully determine which exposure occurs or how the environment responds.

Luck should therefore not be modeled as the opposite of adaptation.

---

## 14. Measurement Bridge

Future measurement should distinguish:

- available signals from perceived signals;
- search effort from search success;
- learning process from outcome;
- coordination capacity from centralized control;
- adaptive capacity from realized adaptation;
- adaptation from improvement;
- discovery from diffusion;
- and selection from superiority.

---

## Conclusion

Adaptive processes translate system capabilities into actions and changing relationships.

Their formalization preserves roles for uncertainty, constraints, environmental response, and feedback. It also prevents realized success, persistence, or survival from being treated as direct evidence of superior capability.
