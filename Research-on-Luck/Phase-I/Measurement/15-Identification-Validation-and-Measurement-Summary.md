# Identification, Validation, and Measurement Summary

## Purpose

This document integrates the additive Measurement series and defines standards for identification, validation, evidence synthesis, and reporting.

Measurement is complete only when indicators, relationships, uncertainty, and alternative explanations are evaluated together.

---

## 1. Identification Target

Every empirical study should state what it seeks to identify:

- description of a state;
- timing of an event;
- exposure;
- association;
- mechanism;
- causal effect;
- feedback;
- trajectory branch;
- or comparative recurrence.

Different targets require different evidence. A descriptive case should not be presented as causal identification.

---

## 2. Construct Validity

Construct validity asks whether indicators represent the intended concept.

Threats include:

- outcome-contaminated capability measures;
- opportunity measured only by realization;
- uncertainty measured only by surprise;
- adaptation measured only by survival;
- and luck measured as residual variation.

Validation should compare alternative operational definitions and inspect discriminant concepts.

---

## 3. Reliability

Reliability concerns consistency across:

- coders;
- sources;
- repeated observations;
- instruments;
- and reasonable coding rules.

High reliability does not guarantee validity. A consistently coded proxy may still measure the wrong construct.

Qualitative coding should document definitions, evidence thresholds, disagreements, and revision procedures.

---

## 4. Causal Identification

Possible strategies include:

- randomized intervention;
- natural experiment;
- discontinuity;
- instrumental variable;
- difference-in-differences;
- matched comparison;
- synthetic control;
- longitudinal process analysis;
- and mechanism-based case comparison.

No strategy is automatically credible. Each requires assumptions concerning assignment, trends, interference, measurement, timing, and missingness.

---

## 5. Mechanism Identification

Mechanism support requires evidence of intermediate links.

For a proposed sequence from exposure to recognition to response to trajectory change, evidence should address each link and competing sequences.

Process tracing may use:

- contemporaneous records;
- timing;
- within-case contrasts;
- expected traces;
- negative evidence;
- and alternative-mechanism tests.

Mechanism evidence and average causal-effect evidence are complementary but distinct.

---

## 6. Feedback Identification

Feedback creates reciprocal relationships and can invalidate simple one-directional models.

Identification may require:

- lagged sequence evidence;
- intervention at different loop points;
- dynamic models;
- network evolution;
- institutional records;
- and evidence of the return path.

A reciprocal correlation alone does not reveal the loop.

---

## 7. Counterfactual Validation

Counterfactual credibility depends on:

- ex ante feasibility;
- comparison quality;
- pre-transition similarity;
- mechanism plausibility;
- sensitivity to assumptions;
- and limited unsupported divergence.

Historical counterfactuals should be graded, not treated as observed outcomes.

---

## 8. Internal and External Validity

### Internal validity

Does the design support the claimed relationship in the studied case or sample?

### External validity

Does the relationship travel to other systems, environments, levels, or periods?

### Comparative validity

Are apparently similar constructs and mechanisms genuinely comparable across domains?

Cross-domain recurrence should be described as candidate generality until boundary conditions are tested.

---

## 9. Triangulation

Evidence is strengthened when independent methods with different weaknesses support the same relationship.

Useful combinations include:

- quantitative trajectory plus archival mechanism evidence;
- network measure plus process tracing;
- institutional coding plus comparative cases;
- experiment plus longitudinal follow-up;
- and simulation plus observed boundary conditions.

Multiple weak measures sharing the same bias do not constitute strong triangulation.

---

## 10. Robustness and Sensitivity

Studies should examine sensitivity to:

- boundary definition;
- level of analysis;
- indicator choice;
- time horizon;
- missing cases;
- survivor selection;
- opportunity classification;
- model specification;
- and counterfactual assumptions.

Results that depend on one arbitrary coding decision should be reported accordingly.

---

## 11. Evidence-Grading Framework

A relationship may be classified as:

1. **Conceptually specified**
2. **Operationalized**
3. **Descriptively observed**
4. **Associated**
5. **Mechanism-supported**
6. **Causally identified within bounds**
7. **Comparatively recurrent**
8. **Candidate for theoretical promotion**

These levels are not automatic stages. Evidence may support one dimension while remaining weak on another.

“Validated” should be reserved for claims with clearly stated scope and substantial supporting evidence.

---

## 12. Luck Inference

A defensible luck inference should assemble evidence for:

- focal system and boundary;
- ex ante information and uncertainty;
- environmental condition;
- interaction or exposure;
- feasible alternatives;
- capability and control;
- response;
- feedback;
- trajectory consequence;
- and evaluative horizon.

Luck should not be inferred from low probability, favorable outcome, unexplained residual, or survival alone.

---

## 13. Minimum Empirical Record

Each study should report:

1. research question;
2. construct definitions;
3. formal relationship;
4. system and level;
5. indicators and sources;
6. time sequence;
7. uncertainty;
8. identification strategy;
9. mechanism evidence;
10. missingness and selection;
11. alternative explanations;
12. counterfactual basis;
13. sensitivity;
14. evidence grade;
15. limitations;
16. revision conditions.

---

## 14. Cross-Domain Application

The five empirical domains are:

- Science;
- Technology;
- Business;
- History;
- Politics.

A shared measurement architecture should enable comparison without requiring identical indicators.

Domain-specific operationalization must preserve the same conceptual role and document departures.

No additional empirical domains are required unless they reveal mechanisms not represented by the current design.

---

## 15. Relationship to General Theory

Measurement does not promote relationships automatically.

Movement toward General Theory requires:

- conceptual precision;
- formal coherence;
- defensible operationalization;
- evidence across relevant cases;
- competing-explanation tests;
- boundary conditions;
- and comparative synthesis.

The additive Measurement series supplies standards for that evaluation. It does not claim that the standards have already been satisfied.

---

## 16. Unresolved Problems

Important unresolved issues include:

- observing unrecognized opportunities;
- reconstructing ex ante information;
- measuring novelty;
- identifying endogenous boundaries;
- separating capability from selection;
- measuring institutional mediation;
- detecting regime change;
- comparing nested levels;
- and estimating counterfactual trajectories with sparse evidence.

These problems should remain explicit research targets.

---

## Conclusion

Measurement converts the architecture into evidence-bearing records and relationship tests.

Its purpose is not to eliminate ambiguity or manufacture a single luck score. It is to distinguish observation from inference, mechanism from correlation, uncertainty from error, and theoretical possibility from empirical support.
