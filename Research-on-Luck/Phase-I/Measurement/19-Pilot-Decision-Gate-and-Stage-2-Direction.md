# Pilot Decision Gate and Stage 2 Direction

## Status

**Step 5 decision record — pilot gate completed; Stage 2A subsequently stopped after reconnaissance**

This record closes the heterogeneous construct-validation pilot decision gate for [Validation Study 1](16-Validation-Study-Recognition-Without-Realization.md). It relies on the completed FLEM-01 Tier 1 cycle, the completed CF-01 Tier 2 controlled replication, and the risk-tiered protocol established in the [FLEM-01 methodological synthesis](18-FLEM-01-Methodological-Synthesis-and-Stage-2-Recommendation.md).

The decision is a **conditional GO**, not a finding that Proposition 4 is validated and not authorization to begin a full empirical study before the Stage 2 sampling frame and feasibility conditions are specified.

## Decision Question

> Has the pilot produced enough construct and procedural information to stop adding heterogeneous validation cases and proceed to the design of a within-domain Stage 2 study?

The gate evaluates whether:

- recognition remains distinguishable from access, pursuit, realization, foreclosure, and later outcome appraisal;
- the instrument can be applied reproducibly to contrasting episodes;
- disagreements can be localized and resolved by explicit prospective rules;
- hindsight controls remain usable;
- and at least one domain offers a plausible route to a defined episode population with comparable evidence.

## Evidence Considered

### FLEM-01 — Delayed realization

FLEM-01 completed the full Tier 1 cycle. Pass A exposed a realization-timing ambiguity and an overlap between Categories 5 and 6. The corrected bounded Pass B design then produced 12/12 candidate-checkpoint classification agreement and 72/72 stage-code agreement.

FLEM-01 therefore provides provisional within-episode evidence that recognition, pursuit, realization timing, and actor attribution can be separated. It does not establish prevalence, causal effect, or cross-domain portability.

### CF-01 — Premature recognition

CF-01 completed the Tier 2 controlled replication under Version 0.2. Pass A produced 18/18 stage-code and 3/3 category agreement. Pass B produced 15/18 strict stage-component agreement and 3/3 final-category agreement. The three candidate claims were classified as premature recognition without categorical falsehood or foreclosure.

The remaining differences concerned allocation of historical recognition versus later evidentiary credibility. They were resolved under the frozen rule and did not require independent audit. CF-01 therefore shows that the instrument can preserve historical recognition while separately evaluating the later standing of the recognized target.

## Gate Assessment

| Criterion | Assessment | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Recognition separable from access, pursuit, and realization | Passed provisionally | FLEM-01 preserved the stage distinctions after prospective correction. |
| Premature recognition distinguishable from categorical falsehood | Passed provisionally | CF-01 retained historical recognition while withholding later validation. |
| Coder disagreement explainable and rule-responsive | Passed | Both cycles localized disagreements; prospective rules produced stable final categories. |
| Hindsight controls operational | Passed provisionally | Both designs froze pre-outcome evidence and recorded later disclosure separately. |

| Within-domain population demonstrably feasible | Not yet passed | This is the first condition for Stage 2 design, not a completed result of the pilot. |

## Decision

The pilot receives a **conditional GO to Stage 2 Science design**.

The pilot has achieved its immediate methodological purpose: it has shown, within two contrasting Science episodes, that the recognition construct can be coded separately from adjacent opportunity stages and that the Version 0.2 review architecture can identify and resolve material ambiguity without requiring every proposed heterogeneous case.

The project should therefore stop treating completion of Betamax, Microsoft, and Kodak as a prerequisite for Stage 2. The next task is to determine whether Science supports a defensible within-domain episode population and evidence regime.

The GO remains conditional on a Stage 2 feasibility and sampling-frame record that:

1. defines the Science episode population independently of realized outcome;
2. states explicit inclusion, exclusion, and episode-boundary rules;
3. demonstrates recoverable contemporaneous or near-contemporaneous evidence across the proposed outcome categories;
4. includes realized, unrealized, rejected, delayed, and false-positive or premature-recognition episodes where the frame permits;
5. specifies comparable pre-outcome cutoffs and outcome horizons;
6. measures authority, resources, capability, and evidence preservation separately from recognition;
7. pre-registers a reliability subsample and exception-review rules under Tier 3;
8. identifies selection, archival, survivorship, and famous-case biases;
9. states stop, narrow, and redesign conditions before substantive coding; and
10. avoids claiming that the pilot validates Proposition 4, causal effects, prevalence, or cross-domain generality.

If these conditions cannot be met, the project should narrow or redesign Stage 2 rather than return automatically to heterogeneous famous-case accumulation.

## Disposition of Remaining Pilot Cases

### Betamax — Deferred

Betamax is deferred. Its main prospective value is portability to Technology and separation of broad opportunity recognition from recognition of selection conditions. That is useful, but it is not necessary to decide whether a Science within-domain design is feasible.

Betamax may be reactivated later only through an explicit portability question, a frozen episode boundary, and a demonstrated need not answerable within Stage 2 Science.

### Microsoft — Reclassified

Microsoft is reclassified from a single heterogeneous pilot case to a possible **within-system episode population**.

Its value lies in comparing multiple bounded opportunity episodes inside one organization across different outcomes, not in assigning one recognition label to “Microsoft.” Any future use should specify episode inclusion rules, organizational levels, decision authority, evidence comparability, and protections against selecting episodes by known outcomes.

Microsoft is therefore not part of the remaining construct-validation pilot queue. It is a candidate design family for a later organizational or within-system study.

### Kodak — Retained conditionally

Kodak is retained conditionally as an optional later portability or boundary-stress test.

It could test the separation of technical awareness, organizational recognition, divided authority, partial pursuit, delayed action, and foreclosure. Those features also create substantial risks of actor-level conflation, retrospective simplification, and outcome-selected narrative.

Kodak should proceed only if Stage 2 Science reveals a specific unresolved construct or actor-boundary question for which Kodak supplies a necessary contrasting test. It is not a prerequisite for the current conditional GO.

## Stage 2 Direction

Stage 2 should begin with **Science feasibility and sampling-frame design**, not immediate case coding.

The preferred unit remains the opportunity episode. The design should seek a bounded population or auditable sampling frame in which discovery, claim, research-program, or development episodes can be identified without first conditioning on success or fame.

The design should compare recognition with subsequent pursuit and realization while treating authority, resources, capability, institutional position, documentation, and evidence survival as competing explanations or selection mechanisms.

## Claims Authorized by This Gate

This decision authorizes the following limited claims:

- the two completed Science episodes support provisional construct separability;
- the controlled replication supports continued use of the Version 0.2 architecture with a prospective clarification separating historical recognition from later claim credibility;
- additional heterogeneous pilot cases are not presently required;
- and Science is the preferred domain for Stage 2 design because it contains the completed validation base.

It does not authorize claims that:

- Proposition 4 is validated;
- recognition causes pursuit or realization;
- the construct is portable across domains;
- the pilot estimates prevalence or effect size;
- or Science necessarily provides a viable episode population.

## Reopening Rules

The pilot decision should be reopened if:

- Stage 2 feasibility work cannot define an outcome-independent Science sampling frame;
- recognition collapses into authority, pursuit, or retrospective credibility under broader sampling;
- evidence availability is systematically conditional on success;
- reliability fails under the Tier 3 subsample;
- or a material portability claim requires a new cross-domain stress test.

Otherwise, the project should proceed through the Stage 2 gate without adding further famous cases by default.

## Final Disposition

**Conditional GO to Stage 2 Science design.**

- **Betamax:** deferred.
- **Microsoft:** reclassified as a possible within-system episode population.
- **Kodak:** retained conditionally as an optional later portability or boundary-stress test.
- **Subsequent disposition:** the bounded reconnaissance is complete. The original Stage 2 Science scaling design is closed; any post-entry redesign requires a separate explicit decision.
