# Opportunity and Constraint Indicators

## Purpose

This document operationalizes opportunity and constraint as dynamic system–environment relationships.

Measurement should distinguish potential, access, recognition, pursuit, realization, partial realization, expiration, and foreclosure.

---

## 1. Unit of Analysis

An opportunity record should identify:

- focal system;
- candidate opportunity;
- relevant environment;
- objective or evaluative standard;
- opening and closing times;
- required interaction;
- and evidence of ex ante feasibility.

The same environmental condition may generate different opportunity records for different systems.

---

## 2. Potential Opportunity

Evidence of potential may include:

- available technology or knowledge;
- institutional eligibility;
- market or scientific opening;
- unoccupied role;
- documented alternative;
- or comparable systems successfully entering the interaction.

Potential should not be inferred solely from ex post success by another system. Feasibility at the relevant time must be established.

---

## 3. Access

Access indicators may include:

- legal eligibility;
- geographic reach;
- network connection;
- information availability;
- financial or material resources;
- required capability;
- organizational authority;
- and timing.

Access is multidimensional. A system may possess formal permission without practical access.

---

## 4. Recognition

Recognition evidence may include:

- contemporaneous statements;
- search behavior;
- internal proposals;
- experiments;
- attention allocation;
- forecasts;
- or discussion of the possibility.

Later claims that an opportunity was “obvious” are not direct evidence of ex ante recognition.

Recognition can be graded as absent, partial, mistaken, delayed, or explicit.

---

## 5. Pursuit

Pursuit indicators include:

- decision;
- resource commitment;
- experiment;
- application;
- partnership;
- entry;
- policy action;
- or repeated search.

Pursuit intensity, duration, reversibility, and opportunity cost may matter.

A decision not to pursue should be evaluated using ex ante constraints and competing objectives.

---

## 6. Realization

Realization should be defined as a specified change in state or accessible trajectory, not merely activity.

Indicators may include:

- discovery confirmed;
- technology adopted;
- market entered;
- institution changed;
- alliance formed;
- resource acquired;
- or constraint removed.

Partial realization should be recorded when only some anticipated change occurs.

---

## 7. Foreclosure and Expiration

Foreclosure evidence may include:

- policy prohibition;
- competing adoption;
- resource depletion;
- missed deadline;
- irreversible commitment;
- network exclusion;
- standard lock-in;
- or environmental transformation.

Expiration reflects closure of a time window; foreclosure emphasizes a process that removed access.

---

## 8. Unrealized Opportunities

Unrealized cases should be classified by stage:

- potential but inaccessible;
- accessible but unrecognized;
- recognized but not pursued;
- pursued but not realized;
- interrupted;
- expired;
- or foreclosed.

This prevents “missed opportunity” from becoming one undifferentiated category.

Evidence should be graded because unrealized alternatives often leave weaker records.

---

## 9. Constraint Indicators

Constraints may be measured through:

- resource thresholds;
- legal restrictions;
- information gaps;
- capability requirements;
- network distance;
- transaction costs;
- institutional vetoes;
- time scarcity;
- political power;
- and physical limits.

A constraint indicator should specify the transition it restricts.

Constraints may enable coordination or protection, so fewer constraints should not automatically be coded as better.

---

## 10. Opportunity Landscape

A system’s opportunity landscape may be represented by:

- number of feasible alternatives;
- diversity of alternatives;
- accessibility distribution;
- expected costs;
- timing windows;
- complementarities;
- and concentration of access.

Counts alone can be misleading if opportunities differ greatly in feasibility or consequence.

Qualitative mapping may be more defensible than a composite index where comparability is weak.

---

## 11. Opportunity Migration

Evidence of migration includes:

- diffusion to other systems;
- imitation;
- changing eligibility;
- regulation;
- resource movement;
- knowledge transfer;
- and competitive displacement.

Measurement should identify whether an opportunity expanded, transferred, or was foreclosed for one system by realization elsewhere.

---

## 12. Institutional Distribution

Institutional measures should examine:

- allocation rules;
- gatekeeping;
- risk sharing;
- access costs;
- recognition systems;
- enforcement;
- and distribution of returns.

Aggregate opportunity growth may coexist with concentrated access.

---

## 13. Counterfactual Feasibility

A proposed unrealized path is stronger when supported by:

- contemporaneous plans;
- available capabilities;
- comparable systems;
- documented alternatives;
- near implementation;
- or a small, defensible change in conditions.

Counterfactuals become weaker as they require multiple unsupported deviations.

---

## 14. Cross-Domain Core

Across Science, Technology, Business, History, and Politics, retain a common stage vocabulary:

Potential → Access → Recognition → Pursuit → Realization or Foreclosure.

Domain-specific indicators may differ, but the conceptual sequence supports comparison.

---

## Conclusion

Opportunity measurement should reconstruct relationships and stages rather than count successful outcomes.

Separating potential, access, recognition, pursuit, realization, and foreclosure makes unrealized alternatives visible and helps distinguish capability, constraint, timing, institutions, and uncertain exposure.
