# Capability and Adaptive Process Indicators

## Purpose

This document operationalizes perception, search, learning, coordination, adaptive capacity, response, adaptation, discovery, innovation, and selection.

Capability must be distinguished from realized performance. Successful outcomes are produced through capability interacting with environment, exposure, institutions, resources, and uncertainty.

---

## 1. Capability Profiles

Capability should normally be represented as a profile rather than one score.

Dimensions may include:

- domain knowledge;
- technical skill;
- information processing;
- search capacity;
- learning;
- coordination;
- resource mobilization;
- experimentation;
- institutional authority;
- and environmental influence.

Indicators should be selected for a specified task and context. General reputation is not a substitute for relevant capability.

---

## 2. Perception

Measure separately:

- signals available;
- signals observed;
- attention allocated;
- interpretation made;
- confidence;
- and delay.

Sources may include logs, correspondence, reports, search records, meeting notes, interviews, and contemporaneous decisions.

Ex post explanation should not be coded as ex ante perception without supporting evidence.

---

## 3. Search

Search indicators may include:

- breadth;
- depth;
- duration;
- frequency;
- diversity of sources;
- experiments conducted;
- resources spent;
- and exploration–exploitation balance.

Search success is an outcome, not the measure of search effort itself.

Opportunity exposure generated by search should be distinguished from passive exposure.

---

## 4. Learning

Learning evidence requires change following information or experience.

Possible indicators include:

- updated beliefs;
- revised models;
- changed routines;
- improved prediction;
- reduced repeated error;
- knowledge retention;
- and transfer to new settings.

A changed outcome without evidence of changed information or behavior does not establish learning.

Learning may also reinforce a mistaken model when evidence is selected or feedback is misleading.

---

## 5. Coordination

Coordination indicators may include:

- information sharing;
- role clarity;
- decision speed;
- alignment of action;
- conflict resolution;
- interoperability;
- and collective resource mobilization.

Centralization is not identical to coordination. Common outcomes may reflect authority, coercion, or shared exposure.

---

## 6. Adaptive Capacity

Adaptive capacity is the feasible ability to change, not the realized change alone.

Evidence may include:

- available alternative routines;
- slack or reserves;
- modularity;
- redundancy;
- decentralized experimentation;
- institutional flexibility;
- response authority;
- and prior demonstrated transfer.

Composite indices should retain dimension-level results and justify weighting.

---

## 7. Response and Non-Response

Record:

- response type;
- timing;
- intensity;
- reversibility;
- resource commitment;
- and stated rationale.

Non-response categories include:

- no recognition;
- deliberate waiting;
- insufficient resources;
- prohibition;
- competing priority;
- inability to coordinate;
- and rejection after evaluation.

This avoids treating every non-response as incapacity.

---

## 8. Adaptation

Adaptation indicators should distinguish:

- behavioral change;
- structural change;
- relational change;
- capability change;
- and environmental modification.

Evaluation should separately assess:

- immediate fit;
- longer-term performance;
- robustness to changed conditions;
- distribution of costs;
- and new vulnerabilities.

Adaptation is not automatically improvement.

---

## 9. Discovery

Discovery evidence may include:

- new observation;
- verified relationship;
- new explanatory model;
- or newly recognized possibility.

Measurement should reconstruct:

- prior knowledge;
- exposure;
- experimentation;
- recognition;
- verification;
- and diffusion.

Priority or fame is not identical to discovery mechanism.

---

## 10. Innovation

Innovation indicators may include:

- new practice or artifact;
- implementation;
- adoption;
- diffusion;
- complementary change;
- and environmental effect.

Invention, implementation, adoption, and impact should remain separate stages.

---

## 11. Selection

Selection measurement should identify:

- unit selected;
- selection environment;
- criterion;
- entry population;
- persistence or replication outcome;
- and observation process.

Survival samples should include non-survivors where possible.

Selection into visibility, documentation, or study must be distinguished from selection in the underlying process.

---

## 12. Capability–Outcome Separation

A stronger design uses independent capability evidence measured before the outcome.

Useful contrasts include:

- similar capability, different exposure;
- similar exposure, different capability;
- similar response, different environmental feedback;
- and repeated performance across different conditions.

Outcome-based capability measures risk circularity.

---

## 13. Cross-Level Capacity

Individual skills, organizational routines, institutional flexibility, and ecosystem diversity are different constructs.

Cross-level claims need mechanisms showing how component capabilities aggregate or fail to aggregate.

An organization may be capable despite individual turnover, or constrained despite highly capable members.

---

## 14. Measurement Uncertainty

Capability and adaptation measures are vulnerable to:

- hindsight;
- survivor selection;
- halo effects;
- inconsistent task definitions;
- changing environments;
- and proxy contamination by outcomes.

Use multiple sources, pre-outcome evidence, negative cases, and sensitivity analysis where possible.

---

## Conclusion

Adaptive-process measurement should observe perception, search, learning, coordination, response, and change directly rather than infer them from success.

This preserves the interaction between capability and luck while preventing survival, visibility, or favorable outcomes from becoming circular evidence of superior capability.
