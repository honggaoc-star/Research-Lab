## Frozen Candidate Claims

The broad label “cold fusion” is too ambiguous for coding. CF-01 therefore separates three candidate claims.

### CF-01A — Reproducible excess-enthalpy phenomenon

> Electrolysis of heavy water using palladium cathodes can produce excess enthalpy beyond adequately measured electrochemical and chemical inputs, and the effect can be reproduced under specified experimental conditions.

### CF-01B — New nuclear-process attribution

> The reported excess enthalpy or associated products arise from a new nuclear process involving deuterium in palladium under near-ambient electrochemical conditions.

### CF-01C — Useful-energy opportunity

> The reported phenomenon provides a technically credible basis for net useful-energy development beyond a laboratory anomaly.

These candidates are nested but not interchangeable. Evidence for an anomalous heat measurement does not by itself establish a nuclear process, and a nuclear anomaly does not by itself establish a useful energy source.

## Prospective Realization Criteria

### CF-01A

Realization requires independent reproduction of excess enthalpy under adequately controlled calorimetry, with disclosed procedures, error analysis, and results sufficiently consistent to distinguish the effect from measurement or electrochemical artifacts.

One laboratory's original report, intermittent unstandardized observations, or an isolated positive result without adequate controls does not satisfy the criterion.

### CF-01B

Realization requires independently reproducible evidence connecting the phenomenon to a nuclear process. Under known deuterium-fusion pathways, claimed heat must be accompanied by nuclear products or radiation at physically commensurate levels. A proposed unknown nuclear pathway requires reproducible nuclear evidence capable of discriminating it from chemical, instrumental, contamination, or background explanations.

### CF-01C

Realization requires CF-01A plus a reproducible net-energy relationship, sufficiently characterized operating conditions, and evidence that the effect can be controlled or scaled beyond an unresolved laboratory observation. Recognition of possible energy implications is not realization.

## Confidence Separation

Coders must report:

- **threshold-realization confidence:** confidence that the frozen criterion was or was not met by the horizon; and
- **causal-effect confidence:** confidence in the inference that the reported effect arose from the claimed process rather than a competing explanation.

The fields must not be collapsed.

## Pass A Interpretation Boundary

The Pass A paper reports observations and advances interpretations. Coders may therefore find recognition and pursuit evidence in the same document that reports claimed experimental effects.

To preserve the validation test:

- realization in Pass A is coded **not yet observable** because the frozen criteria require independent reproduction or assessment;
- the paper's own reported result may support the authors' recognition and pursuit but cannot independently satisfy realization;
- the existence of later negative, positive, or contested evidence is inadmissible in Pass A;
- and Category 8, premature or false recognition, is unavailable in Pass A because it requires later validation evidence.

## Actor and System Boundary

- Fleischmann and Pons's interpretation is not automatically University of Utah institutional recognition.
- University press, patent, funding, or administrative action is not scientific validation.
- An independent laboratory's decision to replicate is recognition of a claim as worth testing, not recognition that the claim is true.
- A replication group's result belongs to that group.
- The DOE panel's assessment is a panel-level synthesis, not a new experimental realization and not the unanimous judgment of all scientists.
- Later persistence of low-energy nuclear-reaction research must not be projected into the 1989 horizon.

## Foreclosure Rule

Rejection, skepticism, absence of special funding, or failure to satisfy realization is not automatically foreclosure.

Foreclosure requires affirmative evidence that the specified candidate became infeasible for the focal system by the horizon. Continuing uncertainty, limited support, or a recommendation for only modest focused research may leave the candidate unrealized without being foreclosed.

## Competing Explanations to Preserve

- calorimetric or electrochemical measurement error;
- incomplete recombination or uncontrolled system boundaries;
- background, contamination, or statistical problems in nuclear-product measurement;
- material-preparation differences affecting reproducibility;
- an anomalous but non-nuclear heat process;
- an unknown nuclear process with products unlike known deuterium fusion;
- inadequate replication fidelity;
- and institutional or publication incentives affecting attention and interpretation.

No competing explanation is pre-adjudicated in the coder packet.

## Feasibility Limitations

- The initial paper contains claimed experimental outcomes, so Pass A is pre-independent-validation rather than pre-observation.
- The authors' complete laboratory notebooks and preannouncement reasoning are not in the packet.
- The DOE report is an authoritative contemporaneous synthesis, not a complete substitute for every underlying laboratory record.
- Some claims remained contested, and the DOE panel did not state that every observation was disproved.
- The useful-energy candidate is more conjunctive and inferential than the two scientific candidates.

These limitations are explicit and do not prevent the bounded test.
