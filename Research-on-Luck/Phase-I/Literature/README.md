# Literature

## Purpose

The **Literature** directory situates **Research on Luck** within existing scholarship. It reviews how disciplines define and investigate luck, uncertainty, randomness, risk, contingency, attribution, opportunity, responsibility, and related phenomena.

Literature does not validate the project’s architecture merely because similar terms appear in prior work. Its role is to identify intellectual sources, competing definitions, unresolved debates, established methods, and claims that the project must distinguish from its own contributions.

## Relationship to the Version 2.1 Architecture

The current architecture begins with System, Environment, Interaction, and Uncertainty and treats luck as relational ex post attribution.

Literature should test and contextualize, not retroactively impose, this vocabulary. In particular, it should examine:

- objective, subjective, and relational theories of luck;
- moral and epistemic luck;
- probability, risk, model uncertainty, and stochasticity;
- attribution, hindsight, control, and deservingness;
- economic opportunity, inequality, and mobility;
- counterfactual and modal accounts;
- contingency and path dependence;
- selection and survivorship;
- and institutional distribution of exposure and consequence.

Different traditions may reject or divide the project’s categories differently. Those disagreements are evidence, not defects to be standardized away.

## Literature Review Standard

Each review should state:

1. its disciplinary question and scope;
2. principal concepts and definitions;
3. influential positions and competing schools;
4. relevant methods and evidence;
5. areas of agreement and disagreement;
6. limits of transfer to this project;
7. implications for formalization and measurement;
8. and unresolved questions.

Claims about what a discipline “generally” believes should be sourced and qualified.

## Boundaries

Literature is not:

- a comprehensive bibliography;
- an authority list;
- an empirical case section;
- a place to present project hypotheses as scholarly consensus;
- or proof of originality.

[References](../References/README.md) manages evidence and citation standards. Foundations defines canonical project terminology. Literature records the scholarly conversation and how the project enters it.

## Long-Term Goal

The goal is an accurate, contestable account of the scholarship relevant to luck attribution and consequential uncertainty. The Literature section should make the project more informed and more constrained—not merely more interdisciplinary in appearance.
