# Statistics

## Overview

Statistics provides the mathematical and methodological foundation for reasoning under uncertainty. While philosophy investigates the conceptual nature of luck and economics examines its influence on observable outcomes, statistics develops the tools for representing uncertainty, drawing inferences from data, and evaluating evidence.

Importantly, statistics does **not** study luck directly. Instead, it studies probability, variation, stochastic processes, estimation, inference, and model uncertainty. These concepts provide the language through which many empirical investigations of luck are conducted.

For the **Research on Luck** project, statistics serves two complementary roles. First, it provides methods for measuring and analyzing uncertain phenomena. Second, it clarifies the distinction between properties of the world and properties of statistical models. This distinction is essential because uncertainty, randomness, residual variation, and measurement error are often mistaken for luck, even though they represent fundamentally different concepts.

Rather than asking *What is luck?* or *How does luck influence outcomes?*, statistics asks:

> **How should uncertainty be represented, quantified, and inferred from data?**

This perspective complements the conceptual foundations established in philosophy and the empirical investigations developed in economics.
