## CF-01B — New nuclear-process attribution

| Field | Coder entry |
|---|---|
| Focal actor or system | **Martin Fleischmann and Stanley Pons as the specified research collaboration in the University of Utah laboratory setting.** |
| Potential: code and reason | **Supported.** The introduction explicitly asks whether deuterium fusion could be feasible in palladium under the reported electrochemical conditions. The reported neutron, gamma-ray, tritium, and heat observations are interpreted as motivating a possible nuclear process. |
| Access: code and reason | **Supported.** The collaboration reports access to palladium–deuterium electrolysis systems, neutron and gamma detection, tritium sampling and scintillation measurement, calorimetry, and comparative platinum blanks. This is access to the claimed evidentiary channels, not proof that the nuclear attribution was correct. |
| Recognition: code and reason | **Supported.** The paper states that sustained high excess enthalpy was “inconceivable” as anything other than nuclear processes, interprets gamma-ray and neutron measurements as confirming neutron generation, identifies accumulated tritium, and concludes that most energy release arose from an unknown nuclear process or processes involving deuterons. The actor, perceived claim, scientific consequence, timing, and passages are explicit. |
| Pursuit: code and reason | **Supported.** Pursuit includes gamma spectroscopy, neutron counting, tritium sampling and scintillation analysis, platinum blank experiments, rate calculations, comparison of nuclear-product rates with heat requirements, theoretical interpretation, publication, and stated further work on the responsible processes. |
| Realization | **Not yet observable under the frozen Pass A rule.** Independent reproducible nuclear evidence discriminating the claim from competing explanations is outside the initial paper. |
| Foreclosure: code and reason | **Not supported.** The collaboration reports open questions and continued work, not affirmative infeasibility. |
| Exact evidence: printed pp. 301–308 | **Printed pp. 301–302:** the paper asks whether deuterium fusion reactions are feasible under high-compression palladium-lattice conditions. **Printed pp. 303–306:** it describes gamma, neutron, and tritium methods and reports neutron counts above background, gamma-ray spectra, and tritium accumulation; it also observes that measured neutron and tritium rates are far below those required to explain the reported enthalpy. **Printed pp. 307–308:** the discussion says that the “bulk of the energy release is due to an hitherto unknown nuclear process or processes” and states that further work is required to establish the responsible processes. |
| Strongest alternative coding | **Contested recognition / Category 3.** The paper’s own calculation shows a many-orders-of-magnitude mismatch between measured neutron/tritium rates and the rates required to explain the heat, leading it to infer unspecified additional nuclear processes. Weak signals, background correction, low counting efficiency, and deferred full details make the evidentiary attribution contestable. I nevertheless code recognition as supported because the collaboration’s contemporaneous nuclear interpretation and directed pursuit are explicit. |
| Provisional recognition category | **6 — Recognition followed by pursuit without realization.** |
| Main ambiguity | Whether recognition of an unspecified “unknown nuclear process” adequately matches the frozen claim requiring a new nuclear process involving deuterium in palladium, given that the specifically measured deuterium-fusion channels were acknowledged to account for only a small fraction of the claimed heat. |
| Threshold-realization confidence | **High.** The frozen rule expressly requires later independent reproducible and discriminating evidence. |
| Causal-effect confidence | **Low.** The authorized source supplies a clear causal claim but also records a major quantitative mismatch and unresolved mechanism; no independent reproduction or discrimination from competing explanations is admissible at Pass A. |

This supports perception of a possible useful-energy opportunity, though not its independent technical credibility. |

| Pursuit: code and reason | **Supported, but less strongly than for 01A and 01B.** The collaboration varied electrode dimensions and current densities, calculated break-even measures, considered scale-up variables, reported a destructive cube experiment, and stated that further work to extend electrode dimensions was in progress.
