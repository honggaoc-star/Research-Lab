## 01B — Selective inhibition as a bacteriological isolation tool

| Field | Coder entry |
|---|---|
| Candidate opportunity | **01B: use of selective inhibition as a bacteriological isolation tool** |
| Potential: code, pages, reason | **Supported — pp. 231–234.** The demonstrated differential sensitivity of bacterial groups made selective suppression possible while permitting insensitive organisms to grow. |
| Access: code, pages, reason | **Supported — pp. 233–234.** Fleming possessed active filtrate, suitable culture media, clinical specimens, and a workable procedure for applying penicillin to plates. |
| Recognition: code, pages, reason | **Supported — pp. 233–236.** Fleming explicitly identified the possibility of suppressing obscuring organisms so that insensitive organisms could develop and be isolated. The specified purpose was bacteriological isolation, especially of *B. influenzae*. This is direct prospective interpretation, not an inference from later use. |
| Pursuit: code, pages, reason | **Supported — pp. 233–235.** Fleming reports laboratory application to mixed cultures, nasal mucus, sputum, and post-nasal swabs from 25 cases. He compared treated and untreated plate regions and described the necessary culture-medium condition. |
| Realization | **Supported — pp. 233–236.** The paper reports repeated isolation of *B. influenzae* from treated cultures when it was missed in untreated cultures and concludes that its value as an isolation aid had been demonstrated. |
| Foreclosure: code, pages, reason | **Not supported.** No closing or loss of access to this opportunity is reported. |
| Relevant actor | Alexander Fleming; Dr. McLean participated in the reported 25-case culture series. |
| Prospective purpose identified | Suppress penicillin-sensitive organisms that mask a target organism, thereby making penicillin-insensitive organisms easier to detect and isolate. |
| Contemporary constraints | The target organism had to be insensitive while competing organisms were sensitive; potency determined the required quantity; an appropriate medium remained necessary; loss of symbiotic support could make colonies very small; some culture sampling was incomplete (pp. 233–235). |
| Strongest alternative coding | **Category 5, partial pursuit**, if “realization” is reserved for routine external adoption rather than successful achievement within Fleming’s laboratory. The packet’s focal-system boundary favors realization within the laboratory. |
| Overall confidence | **High.** |

## 01C — Local therapeutic or antiseptic application

| Field | Coder entry |
|---|---|
| Candidate opportunity | **01C: local therapeutic or antiseptic application against susceptible microbes** |
| Potential: code, pages, reason | **Supported — pp. 231–232, 235–236.** Penicillin inhibited susceptible pathogens at substantial dilution, appeared minimally toxic or irritating in the reported tests, and could be applied directly to infected surfaces. |
| Access: code, pages, reason | **Supported — pp. 228, 232, 235–236.** Fleming had sterile active filtrate and reports local application or irrigation, including infected human surfaces and the conjunctiva. |
| Recognition: code, pages, reason | **Supported — pp. 235–236.** Fleming explicitly identified possible treatment of bacterial infections and suggested antiseptic application to, or injection into, areas infected with susceptible microbes. The prospective purpose was local control or treatment of infection. |
| Pursuit: code, pages, reason | **Supported — pp. 232, 236.** The source reports irrigation of infected human surfaces and says experiments concerning treatment of pyogenic infections were in progress. This supports local therapeutic experimentation, but not demonstrated clinical efficacy or drug-development commitment. |
| Realization | **Not supported.** Local exposure was performed, but the paper does not demonstrate successful treatment of infection. It expressly describes treatment-value experiments as still in progress (p. 236). Safety or non-irritation is not equivalent to therapeutic realization. |
| Foreclosure: code, pages, reason | **Not supported.** No closure is shown. |
| Relevant actor | Alexander Fleming and unspecified participants in the reported local-use experiments. No organizational recognition is inferred. |
| Prospective purpose identified | Use the filtrate as a locally applied antiseptic or treatment against infections involving penicillin-sensitive microbes. |
| Contemporary constraints | Slow bactericidal action; organism-specific susceptibility; no reported controlled efficacy result; changing potency and limited storage stability; crude filtrate rather than purified agent; uncertain effective delivery into infected tissue (pp. 228, 231–232, 235–236). |
| Strongest alternative coding | **Realization supported**, if “application” alone—not effective treatment—is considered realization of the candidate. I reject that interpretation because the candidate is therapeutic or antiseptic application and the paper leaves therapeutic value under investigation. |
| Overall confidence | **Medium.** Recognition and pursuit are clear; the boundary between partial pursuit and realization is ambiguous. |
