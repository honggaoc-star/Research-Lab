# References

## Purpose

The **References** section serves as the long-term bibliographic foundation for the *Research on Luck* project.

Its purpose is to organize and curate the scholarly literature, historical sources, datasets, archival materials, and other references that support the conceptual framework, empirical studies, comparative analyses, and theoretical development presented throughout the repository.

Rather than functioning as a simple bibliography, this section is intended to become a structured reference library that supports future research, validation, and continued refinement of the project.

---

# Current Status

The primary focus of the current development phase has been the construction of the repository's conceptual architecture.

This has included:

* defining foundational concepts,
* reviewing existing scholarship,
* developing formal mechanisms,
* conducting comparative empirical studies,
* and integrating findings into a coherent research framework.

As a result, comprehensive reference management has been intentionally deferred until the conceptual framework reaches greater stability.

---

# Future Development

As the repository matures, this section will evolve into a curated research library supporting every major component of the project.

Potential organization may include:

References may also be organized around recurring adaptive mechanisms, including:

* Opportunity Recognition

* Institutional Adaptation
* Learning

This organization will complement the empirical and comparative structure of the repository.

---

# Design Philosophy

The *Research on Luck* project emphasizes a staged research methodology.

1. Develop the conceptual framework.
2. Validate the framework through comparative empirical evidence.
3. Consolidate and organize supporting scholarly references.
4. Continue refinement through ongoing research and evidence.

The References section therefore represents the long-term evidence infrastructure of the repository rather than a complete bibliography at the current stage of development.

As the research program evolves, this section will become an increasingly important resource for documentation, validation, and future investigation.
