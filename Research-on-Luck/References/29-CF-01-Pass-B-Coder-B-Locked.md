## Hindsight and revision check

1. **Which Pass A recognition judgments remain valid as historical recognition judgments?**  
   All three. Fleischmann and Pons historically recognized and pursued claims corresponding to CF-01A, CF-01B, and CF-01C by the Pass A cutoff. Pass B does not rewrite those actor-level historical judgments.

2. **Which candidate is most strongly undermined by Pass B, and why?**  
   **CF-01B.** The named replication found no nuclear products; the wider record supplied no reproducible discriminating nuclear evidence; known-pathway products were never commensurate with claimed heat; tritium and neutron claims conflicted; helium searches were negative; and materials analysis supplied no known basis for enhancement. CF-01C is also strongly undermined, but principally because its upstream predicates failed.

3. **Does any candidate meet its frozen realization criterion?**  
   **No.** CF-01A lacks adequately controlled independent reproducibility; CF-01B lacks independently reproducible, discriminating, commensurate nuclear evidence; CF-01C lacks reproducible net energy, characterized conditions, control/stability/scale, and a credible pathway.

4. **Does any candidate meet the affirmative foreclosure rule?**  
   **No.** The panel's negative assessment and funding recommendation are not closing conditions. It left unresolved questions and recommended modest focused work.

5. **Is Category 8 warranted for any candidate? Distinguish “premature” from “false” where possible.**  
   **Yes, for all three, as premature recognition.** The later bounded evidence materially undermines each recognized claim under its frozen definition. It does not establish fraud, impossibility, categorical falsehood, or permanent foreclosure. The case for Category 8 is strongest for CF-01B and CF-01C; CF-01A's strongest alternative is Category 3 because unexplained bursts and conflicting heat reports remained.

6. **What uncertainty remains at the frozen horizon?**  
   The causes of sporadic heat excursions and occasional low-level product reports remained unresolved; positive and negative protocols were not cleanly differentiated; some observations were not invalidated; and the panel could not categorically prove or disprove every claim. None of that uncertainty satisfies a frozen realization criterion.

7. **Would any Pass B code change if evidence after 26 November 1989 were unknown?**  
   **No.** The codes are based only on my locked Pass A record, CF-B01, and CF-B02 through the frozen horizon.
