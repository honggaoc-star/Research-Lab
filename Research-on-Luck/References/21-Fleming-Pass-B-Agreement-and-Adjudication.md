### Terminology disposition

At B1, Alpha wrote “supported only in infected mice; insufficient for full 01D realization,” while Beta wrote “partially supported.” This is a terminology difference rather than an evidentiary disagreement. Both records identify the same facts and limitation: systemic administration with reported therapeutic effect existed in mice, but the human systemic-effect threshold required for the integrated medicine trajectory had not been reached.

For cross-record comparison, the harmonized status is:

> **Preclinical partial support; full human systemic-administration-and-effect component not supported at B1.**

Neither locked record is edited.

## Methodological Findings

The Fleming two-pass cycle supports five bounded conclusions:

1. Recognition, pursuit, realization, and actor attribution can be coded separately across sequential checkpoints.
2. Later realization need not enlarge the original actor's recognition target.
3. A candidate may move across focal systems, with different systems supplying distinct realization components.
4. Conjunctive candidates require component-level coding; a favorable animal or clinical result alone does not establish a stable, purified, scalable medicine.
5. Sequential release successfully preserved the distinction between B1 preclinical evidence, B2 bounded clinical evidence, and B3 operational scale.

## Rule Revisions for Future Packets

Before applying this design to another episode:

- define whether a component status may use “partial support” or must use only the standard five evidence codes;
- define an explicit decision test for “operationally consequential scale”;
- distinguish threshold realization confidence from causal-effect confidence;
- pre-register treatment of accidental exposure to duplicated protocol language;
- keep coder-thread setup isolated from audit discussions; and
- specify whether complete records incorporate prior locked checkpoint files or reference them as an immutable record suite.
