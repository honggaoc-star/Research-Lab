## Coding Approach

Each candidate opportunity was coded separately. Recognition was not inferred merely from observation, naming, experimentation, publication, or later historical importance. A supported recognition code required a specified possibility, a prospective purpose or consequence, a relevant actor, timing within the cutoff, and recoverable support in the supplied paper.

“Not supported” means that the 1929 paper does not provide adequate affirmative evidence for the specified stage. It does not establish historical absence.

## 01A — Scientific Characterization of the Antibacterial Substance

| Field | Coder entry |
|---|---|
| Candidate opportunity | **01A: scientific characterization of the antibacterial substance** |
| Potential: code, pages, reason | **Supported — pp. 226–236.** The observed lysis and diffusible antibacterial activity created a researchable phenomenon (p. 226). The paper subsequently reports differentiating characteristics, assay methods, heat response, filtration, solubility, production and decay, microbial selectivity, rate of killing, and toxicity (pp. 227–236). |
| Access: code, pages, reason | **Supported — p. 226.** Fleming observed the contaminated plate, made subcultures of the mould, and obtained active broth cultures, giving him direct experimental access to the phenomenon and material. |
| Recognition: code, pages, reason | **Supported — p. 226.** Fleming states that experiments were conducted “with a view to ascertaining something of the properties” of the bacteriolytic substance. This statement supplies the actor, interpretation, prospective scientific purpose, and cutoff timing. The recognition inference is carried by the explicitly stated investigative purpose, not merely by the initial observation or later publication. |
| Pursuit: code, pages, reason | **Supported — pp. 226–236.** The paper reports a sustained program of investigation: mould comparison and naming (pp. 226–227), assay methods (p. 227), physical and production properties (pp. 228–231), microbial selectivity (pp. 229–231), killing rate and toxicity (p. 232), and summarized findings (p. 236). This is investigation and characterization, not an inference of organizational drug-development commitment. |
| Realization | **Supported within the cutoff — pp. 226–236.** A substantial scientific characterization was completed and reported. Realization is bounded to the candidate definition: characterization of the antibacterial substance, not purification, structural identification, systemic efficacy, or scalable manufacture. |
| Foreclosure: code, pages, reason | **Not supported.** The packet provides no adequate evidence that further characterization was closed or rendered unavailable. The reported instability and remaining unknowns are constraints, not foreclosure. |
| Relevant actor | Alexander Fleming as investigator in the St Mary’s Hospital Inoculation Department; colleagues are mentioned as assisting with specified experiments, but no organizational recognition is inferred. |
| Prospective purpose identified | To ascertain the properties of the bacteriolytic substance formed in the mould culture (p. 226). |
| Contemporary constraints | The active substance was operationally defined through mould broth filtrate rather than isolated as a purified compound (p. 227); antibacterial power diminished as cultures aged and during storage, with pH-dependent stability (p. 228); activity varied greatly across microbes (pp. 229–231); killing was slow rather than immediate (p. 232); and the paper did not establish chemical identity, complete purification, or systemic efficacy. |
| Strongest alternative coding | **Category 6, recognition followed by pursuit without realization**, if “scientific characterization” is interpreted to require isolation or a substantially complete chemical characterization. I reject that broader interpretation because the candidate says characterization, and the paper reports multiple experimentally established properties while leaving limitations explicit. |
| Overall confidence | **High** |

### 01A Final Classification

**Category 7 — recognition followed by realization within the cutoff.**

## 01B — Use of Selective Inhibition as a Bacteriological Isolation Tool

| Field | Coder entry |
|---|---|
| Candidate opportunity | **01B: use of selective inhibition as a bacteriological isolation tool** |
| Potential: code, pages, reason | **Supported — pp. 229–231.** The reported differential sensitivity—strong inhibition of pyogenic cocci and lack of inhibition of *B. influenzae* and several other organisms—made selective suppression and isolation experimentally possible. |
| Access: code, pages, reason | **Supported — pp. 227, 233–234.** Fleming had active penicillin preparations and workable plate methods. The paper describes adding penicillin to culture media or spreading drops over inoculated plates, providing direct access to the selective-inhibition mechanism. |
| Recognition: code, pages, reason | **Supported — pp. 233–234.** Fleming explicitly identifies the possibility that, when a target pathogen is insensitive and obscuring microbes are sensitive, penicillin can inhibit the latter and allow the former to develop. The stated prospective purpose is easier isolation of *B. influenzae* and potentially other organisms. The recognition inference is carried by this explicit methodological interpretation, not merely by the selectivity tables. |
| Pursuit: code, pages, reason | **Supported — pp. 233–235.** The method was applied to constructed mixed cultures, nasal mucus, sputum, and post-nasal swabs, including a series involving 25 nurses. This is laboratory application of selective inhibition. |
| Realization | **Supported within the cutoff — pp. 233–235.** The paper reports repeated isolation of *B. influenzae* where it was missed in untreated cultures and concludes that the method makes isolation much easier. This realizes the opportunity as a bacteriological isolation tool within the paper’s experimental boundary. |
| Foreclosure: code, pages, reason | **Not supported.** No evidence establishes closure of this methodological opportunity. |
| Relevant actor | Alexander Fleming as investigator; Dr. McLean participated in the reported series of throat cultures (p. 234). This does not establish higher-level organizational recognition. |
| Prospective purpose identified | Suppress penicillin-sensitive organisms that obscure a penicillin-insensitive target so the target can be isolated more readily (pp. 233–234). |
| Contemporary constraints | The target organism had to be penicillin-insensitive while competitors were sensitive (p. 233); an appropriate growth medium remained necessary because suppression could remove symbiotic support (p. 234); some Gram-negative cocci and other organisms also remained uninhibited (pp. 233–235); and thick or confluent cultures limited completeness of some observations (p. 235). |
| Strongest alternative coding | **Category 5, recognition followed by delayed or partial pursuit**, if the reported applications are treated only as preliminary demonstrations. I reject that coding because the candidate is the use of selective inhibition as an isolation tool, and the paper reports actual repeated use and successful isolation within the cutoff. |
| Overall confidence | **High** |

### 01B Final Classification

**Category 7 — recognition followed by realization within the cutoff.**

## 01C — Local Therapeutic or Antiseptic Application Against Susceptible Microbes

| Field | Coder entry |
|---|---|
| Candidate opportunity | **01C: local therapeutic or antiseptic application against susceptible microbes** |
| Potential: code, pages, reason | **Supported — pp. 232, 235–236.** Penicillin inhibited susceptible pathogens at substantial dilution, appeared to have low animal toxicity, did not irritate tested human surfaces, and did not impair leucocytic function more than ordinary broth (p. 232). Fleming explicitly compares it favorably with chemical antiseptics for infections involving sensitive organisms (pp. 235–236). These findings support potential, not demonstrated clinical efficacy. |
| Access: code, pages, reason | **Supported — pp. 228, 232.** Sterile active mould broth could be obtained by filtration (p. 228), and the paper reports irrigation of infected human surfaces and the human conjunctiva as well as animal administration (p. 232). This establishes access to material and local application routes within the experimental setting. |
| Recognition: code, pages, reason | **Supported — pp. 235–236.** Fleming identifies possible treatment of bacterial infections and proposes antiseptic application to infected surfaces; the summary also suggests application to, or injection into, areas infected with susceptible microbes (p. 236). The recognition inference is carried by these prospective treatment statements. |
| Pursuit: code, pages, reason | **Supported — pp. 232, 236.** The paper reports local irrigation and irritation observations (p. 232) and states that experiments concerning treatment of pyogenic infections were in progress (p. 236). This supports local therapeutic experimentation, but not demonstrated therapeutic success or a broader development program. |
| Realization | **Not yet observable.** The paper establishes antibacterial activity, preliminary tolerability, actual local exposure, and experiments in progress, but it does not report clinical efficacy outcomes sufficient to show realization of treatment or antiseptic benefit. |
| Foreclosure: code, pages, reason | **Not supported.** The paper identifies limitations and ongoing experiments, not closure. |
| Relevant actor | Alexander Fleming as investigator. Human subjects and animals appear in toxicity or irritation observations, but no organizational therapeutic program is established. |
| Prospective purpose identified | Local treatment or antiseptic use against infections involving penicillin-sensitive microbes (pp. 235–236). |
| Contemporary constraints | Selectivity excluded many organisms (pp. 229–231); penicillin killed staphylococci slowly and required hours even at concentrations above the inhibitory level (p. 232); antibacterial power declined during storage and depended on conditions (p. 228); the paper reports limited toxicity and irritation evidence rather than systematic safety evaluation (p. 232); and efficacy, standardized potency, purification, delivery, and scale were not established. |
| Strongest alternative coding | **Category 5, recognition followed by delayed or partial pursuit**, because local irrigation had occurred and treatment experiments were in progress. I prefer Category 6 because pursuit is affirmatively documented but realization of therapeutic efficacy is not. The distinction depends on whether “partial pursuit” in Category 5 is meant to include preliminary treatment experimentation rather than delayed pursuit. |
| Overall confidence | **Medium-high** |

### 01C Final Classification

**Category 6 — recognition followed by pursuit without realization.**

| Field | Coder entry |
|---|---|
| Candidate opportunity | **01D: development as a stable, purified, systemically administered, scalable medicine** |
| Potential: code, pages, reason | **Not supported.** The paper establishes antibacterial activity, preliminary toxicity observations, and limited extraction information, but it does not establish the potential of the full bundled candidate: a stable, purified, systemically administered, scalable medicine. The paper instead reports loss of activity during culture aging and storage (pp. 228, 236), describes penicillin as mould broth filtrate (p. 227), and provides no evidence of scalable production or systemic therapeutic efficacy. |
| Access: code, pages, reason | **Not supported.** Fleming had access to active mould broth filtrate and preliminary extraction procedures, but the source does not establish access to a stable, purified, systemically deliverable, scalable medicinal form. |
| Recognition: code, pages, reason | **Not supported.** The paper recognizes possible local application or injection into infected areas (p. 236), but it does not interpret the specified opportunity as development of a stable, purified, systemically administered, scalable medicine. Recognition of local treatment does not entail recognition of this materially different candidate. |
| Pursuit: code, pages, reason | **Not supported.** No drug-development commitment, purification program, systemic therapeutic program, standardized production process, scale effort, budget, staffing, or organizational authorization is established by the source. Preliminary intravenous toxicity exposure in a rabbit (p. 232) does not by itself demonstrate pursuit of systemic medicinal development. |
| Realization | **Not yet observable.** The candidate was not realized within the supplied 1929 paper. |
| Foreclosure: code, pages, reason | **Not supported.** Instability and missing capabilities constrain the candidate, but the source does not establish that it was permanently or deliberately foreclosed. |
| Relevant actor | Alexander Fleming as investigator. No relevant higher-level drug-development actor can be coded within the fixed system boundary. |
| Prospective purpose identified | **Not supported for the candidate as defined.** The source identifies local treatment and injection into infected areas (pp. 235–236), not stable purified systemic and scalable medicinal development. |

## Cross-Opportunity Distinctions

1. Characterization of the antibacterial substance does not establish recognition of a systemic medicine.
2. Demonstration of selective inhibition and successful bacterial isolation realizes 01B without implying therapeutic realization.
3. Preliminary tolerability and local application support 01C potential, recognition, and pursuit, but do not demonstrate efficacy.
4. Intravenous toxicity exposure in a rabbit is not treated as recognition or pursuit of systemic drug development.
5. Fleming’s statements are coded at the investigator level only; no organizational or industry-level recognition is inferred.
