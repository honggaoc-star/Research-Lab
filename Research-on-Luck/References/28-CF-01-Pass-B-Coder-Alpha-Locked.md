## Hindsight and revision check

1. **Which Pass A recognition judgments remain valid as historical recognition judgments?**  
   All three. Fleischmann and Pons historically recognized and pursued excess enthalpy, a new nuclear attribution, and a useful-energy opportunity by the Pass A cutoff. Pass B does not rewrite those actor-level facts.

2. **Which candidate is most strongly undermined by Pass B, and why?**  
   **CF-01C.** The useful-energy candidate is conjunctive and depends on CF-01A plus repeatable conditions, control or stability, scale, and a credible pathway. Pass B supplied none of those additional elements, while the panel expressly found no convincing evidence that useful energy would result. CF-01B is also strongly undermined by absent commensurate and reproducible nuclear evidence.

3. **Does any candidate meet its frozen realization criterion?**  
   **No.**

4. **Does any candidate meet the affirmative foreclosure rule?**  
   **No.** Negative replications, panel skepticism, and the recommendation against special funding are not affirmative closure; focused investigation remained available.

5. **Is Category 8 warranted for any candidate? Distinguish “premature” from “false” where possible.**  
   **Yes, for CF-01A, CF-01B, and CF-01C as premature recognition.** The later bounded evidence materially undermines each recognized claim under its frozen definition. It does not establish fraud, categorical impossibility, universal falsity, or foreclosure.

6. **What uncertainty remains at the frozen horizon?**  
   Sporadic heat excursions and occasional low-level product reports remained unexplained; the underlying causes were unresolved; experimental conditions were inconsistent; and the panel could not categorically prove or disprove every claim.

7. **Would any Pass B code change if evidence after 26 November 1989 were unknown?**  
   **No.** These codes use only the locked Pass A record and the two authorized Pass B sources through the frozen horizon.
