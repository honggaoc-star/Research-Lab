# Proposition-to-Evidence Map — Tranche 1

## Scope

This first evidence map covers four propositions from [General Theory Version 2.1](../General%20Theory/01-Core-Propositions.md):

- Proposition 4 — Opportunity Recognition Is Uneven
- Proposition 6 — Adaptive Capacity May Mediate Responses
- Proposition 7 — Path Dependence Requires a Reinforcing Mechanism
- Proposition 19 — Comparative Recurrence Motivates but Does Not Prove Generality

The tranche was selected because these propositions expose four central risks: hindsight reconstruction, capability–outcome conflation, historical-sequence explanations, and premature cross-domain generalization.

## Evidence Status

This is an **internal evidence map**, not a completed literature or source review. It maps claims to repository cases and identifies what those cases can and cannot presently support.

Most case documents are interpretive narratives. They generate candidate mechanisms and counterexamples but generally do not yet provide independent identification, representative sampling, or complete claim-level citations.

## Status Scale

| Status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Retain — analytical | The distinction is coherent and useful, but is not itself a causal result |
| Retain — hypothesis | The relationship is plausible and testable; current evidence is suggestive |
| Narrow | Current wording exceeds the evidence or needs tighter boundary conditions |
| Test priority | The proposition should receive bounded formal, measurement, and empirical work |
| Reclassify | The proposition belongs primarily to methodology rather than causal theory |
| Reject | Current evidence contradicts the proposition sufficiently to remove it |

# Proposition 4 — Opportunity Recognition Is Uneven

## Claim

Systems exposed to similar conditions may differ in what they perceive and interpret as actionable. Recognition must be evidenced contemporaneously and should not be inferred from later success.

## Supporting and Limiting Evidence

| Case | Evidentiary role | What it suggests | What it does not establish |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Fleming and Penicillin](../Empirical%20Studies/Science/01-Alexander-Fleming-and-Penicillin.md) | Illustrative support | An anomalous observation required interpretation, prior knowledge, and later institutional development | That Fleming’s recognition was uniquely capable, sufficient for realization, or representative of scientific discovery |
| [Cold Fusion](../Empirical%20Studies/Science/11-Cold-Fusion.md) | Negative and boundary case | Recognition may be premature or false; validation is distinct from recognition | That recognition generally improves outcomes |
| [Betamax](../Empirical%20Studies/Technology/10-Betamax.md) | Recognition without realization | Sony recognized home-video potential but may have misread the conditions governing selection | That later market selection proves inferior recognition or capability |
| [Microsoft](../Empirical%20Studies/Business/04-Microsoft.md) | Within-system variation | Recognition and pursuit varied across enterprise, cloud, mobile, search, and social-network opportunities | That successful transitions identify a stable recognition capability |
| [Kodak](../Empirical%20Studies/Business/06-Kodak.md) | Level and stage distinction | Technical awareness existed, while commercial interpretation and coordinated pursuit differed across organizational levels | That Kodak simply failed to recognize one obvious digital opportunity |

## Competing Explanations

Observed recognition differences may reflect:

- unequal information access;
- retrospective coding of successful ideas as recognition;
- differences in authority or resources rather than perception;
- strategic disagreement about uncertain value;
- false-positive recognition;
- institutional incentives;
- or selection of famous cases.

## Missing Evidence

Priority evidence includes dated proposals, laboratory records, internal memoranda, rejected alternatives, resource commitments, decision rights, and comparable actors exposed to similar information.

## Current Judgment

**Retain — hypothesis; test priority.**

Uneven recognition is plausible and the negative cases make the construct more discriminating. The current cases do not estimate its prevalence or independent effect on realization.

# Proposition 6 — Adaptive Capacity May Mediate Responses

## Claim

Perception, search, learning, coordination, error correction, and resource redeployment may affect responses to changing conditions. Capability must be measured independently of outcomes.

## Supporting and Limiting Evidence

| Case | Evidentiary role | What it suggests | Main identification problem |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Microsoft](../Empirical%20Studies/Business/04-Microsoft.md) | Repeated-transition candidate | Resource redeployment and organizational change may have mattered across several transitions | Success, installed-base power, acquisitions, and competitor error select the observed trajectory |
| [Berkshire Hathaway](../Empirical%20Studies/Business/05-Berkshire-Hathaway.md) | Candidate allocation capability | Liquidity, governance, and decision rules may expand response options | Returns and survival cannot identify capability apart from market regime, capital access, reputation, and survivorship |
| [Kodak](../Empirical%20Studies/Business/06-Kodak.md) | Construct-separation case | Technical capability can coexist with weak coordination or business-model realization | Decline cannot prove general incapability |
| [Democratic Transitions](../Empirical%20Studies/Politics/04-Democratic-Transitions.md) | Selection warning | Institutional processes may affect transition trajectories | Cases labeled successful are selected on later regime survival |
| [China’s Reform and Opening-Up](../Empirical%20Studies/Politics/06-China%27s-Reform-and-Opening.md) | Experimentation candidate | Search, local variation, feedback, and resource redeployment are plausible adaptive processes | Growth, continuity, privileged pilots, reporting selection, coercion, and international conditions confound inference |
| [Black Death](../Empirical%20Studies/History/01-The-Black-Death.md) | Exposure and survivor warning | Institutional responses varied across regions facing demographic shock | Later recovery excludes the dead and may underrepresent displaced groups |

## Competing Explanations

- favorable exposure;
- inherited resources or institutional privilege;
- selection among firms, policies, and regimes;
- environmental stability;
- coercive capacity;
- competitor failure;
- resource abundance;
- and retrospective attribution of process quality from outcomes.

## Missing Evidence

The proposition requires indicators of search, experimentation, feedback use, error correction, coordination, and redeployment before outcomes are known, plus failed initiatives and matched comparison systems.

## Current Judgment

**Retain — hypothesis; highest test priority; do not promote.**

The cases demonstrate why capability must be separated from outcomes more strongly than they demonstrate that a common adaptive-capacity construct mediates trajectories across domains.

# Proposition 7 — Path Dependence Requires a Reinforcing Mechanism

## Claim

Historical sequence becomes path dependence only when an earlier transition changes later probabilities or feasible choices through an identified reinforcing mechanism.

## Supporting and Limiting Evidence

| Case | Candidate mechanism | Evidentiary contribution | Limitation |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Betamax](../Empirical%20Studies/Technology/10-Betamax.md) | Complement availability, licensing, installed base, and network feedback | Illustrates how technological selection may become self-reinforcing | Requires stronger comparative evidence on timing and competing format decisions |
| [Linux](../Empirical%20Studies/Technology/12-Linux.md) | Distributed contribution, reuse, adoption, and complementary infrastructure | Suggests recursive expansion of participation and capability | Later success may overstate inevitability and underrepresent failed open projects |
| [Kodak](../Empirical%20Studies/Business/06-Kodak.md) | Revenue dependence, infrastructure, incentives, and organizational specialization | Shows how earlier success may constrain later pursuit | Multiple mechanisms are bundled and their relative weights are unknown |
| [Industrial Revolution](../Empirical%20Studies/History/02-The-Industrial-Revolution.md) | Complementarity, infrastructure, institutions, and cumulative advantage | Identifies possible reinforcing relations across technologies and regions | The historical unit and time boundary are heterogeneous |
| [United States Constitution](../Empirical%20Studies/Politics/01-The-United-States-Constitution.md) | Replacement costs, accumulated authority, vetoes, and institutional feedback | Distinguishes persistence from proof of constitutional superiority | Endurance is consistent with several non-adaptive explanations |
| [Cold Fusion](../Empirical%20Studies/Science/11-Cold-Fusion.md) | Replication and corrective institutional feedback | Provides a countervailing process that attenuated an initially amplified claim | Does not show whether the same correction generalizes across scientific fields |

## Competing Explanations

Simple persistence may reflect stable external conditions, coercion, efficiency, resource advantage, or lack of alternatives rather than path dependence. Historical sequence without a loop is insufficient.

## Missing Evidence

Each application needs a stated state variable, feedback loop, sign, delay, threshold, reversal condition, and feasible comparison trajectory.

## Current Judgment

**Retain — analytical and relational hypothesis.**

The requirement for a reinforcing mechanism is conceptually strong. Individual mechanisms remain case-specific and require formal and empirical evaluation.

# Proposition 19 — Comparative Recurrence Motivates but Does Not Prove Generality

## Claim

Similar labels across domains may motivate theory but do not establish identical constructs, mechanisms, measurements, or effects.

## Evidence for the Safeguard

The same terms recur in Science, Technology, Business, History, and Politics, including recognition, learning, institutions, adaptation, feedback, and path dependence. Yet the cases map them to different actors, levels, time scales, evidence, and evaluative criteria.

Examples include:

- scientific recognition may be an individual or community-level interpretation requiring replication;
- technological selection may involve standards, complements, and network adoption;
- business adaptation may involve managerial authority and resource redeployment;
- historical adaptation may be an aggregate description rather than a unified system capability;
- political adaptation may involve coercion, legitimacy, distribution, and contested objectives.

## Dependence Problem

The apparent recurrence is not fully independent evidence because:

- cases were selected using a shared conceptual framework;
- documents were written and revised with common terminology;
- many cases are famous or surviving examples;
- domain syntheses aggregate interpretations rather than independent estimates;
- and negative cases are unevenly developed.

Recurrence may therefore reflect genuine similarity, common coding, case selection, or some combination.

## Current Judgment

**Retain — methodological commitment; reclassify rather than test as a causal proposition.**

Proposition 19 should govern promotion of other propositions. It is not itself evidence that a general mechanism exists.

# Recommended Validation Sequence

## Study 1 — Recognition without Realization

Compare recognized opportunities that were pursued, rejected, delayed, falsely perceived, or foreclosed. Use contemporaneous evidence and keep access, authority, and resource constraints separate from recognition.

## Study 2 — Capability before Outcome

Choose one domain and construct capability indicators before classifying outcomes. Include failed and surviving systems and test whether the indicators predict response across more than one transition.

## Study 3 — One Feedback Loop

Select a single path-dependence mechanism—such as format network effects or organizational revenue dependence—and specify its state variables, feedback sign, time delays, reversal conditions, and counterfactual comparison.

## Study 4 — Independent Cross-Domain Coding

Have domain-specific evidence coded without imposing the project’s preferred labels, then assess whether comparable constructs and relationships emerge. This is necessary before using recurrence to support General Theory.

# Tranche 1 Conclusion

The first map supports retaining all four propositions, but not at equal theoretical rank.

- Proposition 4 remains a plausible relational hypothesis.
- Proposition 6 is important but presently underidentified and especially vulnerable to survivor and outcome selection.
- Proposition 7 contains a strong analytical requirement, while its substantive mechanisms remain case-specific.
- Proposition 19 is best treated as a methodological constraint on theory promotion.

The immediate research value lies in narrowing and testing these propositions, not accumulating additional illustrative cases.
