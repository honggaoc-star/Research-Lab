# Conceptual Summary

## Purpose

This document summarizes the new conceptual architecture introduced in Foundations files 10–14. It provides a bridge from canonical concepts to Formalization, Measurement, Empirical Studies, and General Theory.

It does not replace the detailed documents or claim that the architecture is final.

---

## 1. Working Primitives

The architecture begins with four working primitives:

1. **System** — an analytically bounded configuration that persists or changes through time.
2. **Environment** — relevant conditions, entities, structures, and processes outside the focal system boundary.
3. **Interaction** — a relation or process through which systems and environments affect one another.
4. **Uncertainty** — incomplete determination, knowledge, prediction, or control concerning relevant states, interactions, alternatives, or consequences.

Luck is not a primitive.

---

## 2. Layered Architecture

Layer 0 — Working Primitives: System, Environment, Interaction, Uncertainty.

Layer 1 — Derived Conditions: Opportunity Landscape, Constraint, Information, Resources, Variation.

Layer 2 — System Capabilities: Perception, Learning, Search, Adaptive Capacity, Coordination.

Layer 3 — Dynamic Processes: Selection, Adaptation, Discovery, Innovation.

Layer 4 — Long-Term Effects: Evolution, Path Dependence, Resilience, Transformation.

Layer 5 — Outcome Evaluation: Realized Opportunity, Good Luck, Bad Luck, Missed Opportunity, Unexpected Discovery.

The layers do not imply a universal one-way sequence. Feedback can connect every layer, and systems may alter their environments.

---

## 3. Derived Conditions and Capabilities

Opportunities and constraints are relations between systems and environments.

Information, resources, and variation may be properties of systems, features of environments, or contents transmitted and changed through interaction. An opportunity landscape is the structured set of possible and constrained interactions relevant to a system at a time.

Capabilities affect what a system can perceive, access, learn, search for, coordinate, and adapt to. They mediate the conversion of exposure into response but do not fully determine outcomes.

Capability may itself be shaped by prior luck, institutions, resources, learning, and path-dependent feedback.

---

## 4. Dynamic Processes and Long-Term Effects

Interaction produces exposure, recognition, response, experimentation, learning, selection, adaptation, discovery, innovation, and institutionalization.

A mechanism should state how a process changes a system, environment, or relationship.

Repeated interactions may amplify or attenuate differences, stabilize paths, reverse initial effects, alter resilience, transform system structure, or change the environment. Evolution and path dependence require specified mechanisms rather than labels that replace explanation.

---

## 5. Luck Sequence

### Ex ante — Luck Potential

A system occupies an environment containing uncertain exposures, opportunities, constraints, and possible interactions.

### Transition — Luck Realization

Interaction, recognition, response, feedback, and adaptation convert some possibilities into trajectory changes while others remain unrealized or are foreclosed.

### Ex post — Luck Attribution

The realized path is evaluated as good luck, bad luck, missed opportunity, avoided harm, or unexpected discovery.

This sequence guards against projecting ex post knowledge backward.

---

## 6. Working Conception of Luck

> **Luck is consequential variation in a system’s state, relationships, accessible opportunities, constraints, or trajectory arising through interactions under uncertainty and not fully determined by the system’s prior intentions, capabilities, or actions.**

This conception is system-relative, interaction-mediated, uncertainty-dependent, consequence-sensitive, compatible with partial agency, and trajectory-oriented.

It is not equivalent to randomness, risk, surprise, favorable outcome, lack of complete control, or unexplained residual variation.

---

## 7. Minimum Case Specification

A case should identify:

1. focal system and boundary;
2. level of analysis;
3. prior state;
4. relevant environment;
5. uncertainty ex ante;
6. consequential interaction or exposure;
7. recognition and response;
8. opportunities and constraints;
9. feedback and adaptation;
10. trajectory change;
11. plausible unrealized alternatives;
12. evaluative standpoint and horizon.

This prevents outcome-only narratives and unsupported attribution.

---

## 8. Repository Mapping

**Foundations** defines canonical concepts, assumptions, and boundaries.

**Formalization** specifies relationship structures, mechanisms, states, transitions, and testable propositions.

**Measurement** operationalizes systems, environments, interactions, uncertainty, opportunity, capability, and trajectory.

**Empirical Studies** tests the architecture in Science, Technology, Business, History, and Politics.

**Comparative Studies** identifies recurring relationships, differences, and boundary conditions across domains.

**General Theory** promotes only relationships that survive sufficient conceptual, formal, measurement, and comparative scrutiny.

The flow is recursive: evidence may revise foundations.

---

## 10. Current Boundaries

The architecture does not yet establish:

- that the four primitives are complete;
- that every domain uses identical mechanisms;
- that opportunity can always be observed;
- that unrealized trajectories can always be identified;
- that adaptation is necessarily beneficial;
- or that recurring relationships have been validated as general theory.

Institutions, power, agency, information, resources, time, and population structure remain important tests of whether the primitive set is sufficient.

---

## 11. Next Research Steps

The current implementation sequence is:

1. complete Figure 1, **Conceptual Architecture of Adaptive Systems Under Uncertainty**;
2. organize Formalization around relationships among the primitives and derived layers;
3. revise Measurement to operationalize those relationships;
4. audit empirical cases against the architecture;
5. compare mechanisms and boundary conditions across domains;
6. rewrite General Theory around relationships that survive testing.

---

## Conclusion

The new architecture reframes the Research on Luck project from a collection of luck-related concepts into a research program about adaptive systems interacting with changing environments under uncertainty.

Luck remains central, but as an ex post and trajectory-sensitive account of consequential variation—not as the primitive from which every other concept is derived.
