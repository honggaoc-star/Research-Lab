# Research Positioning

## Purpose

**Research on Luck** is a comparative and interdisciplinary research program examining how consequential uncertainty affects bounded system trajectories and when ex post luck attribution is warranted.

It does not present a finished theory, compete with established disciplines, or treat luck as an independent causal force. Its distinctive aim is to connect conceptual analysis, relationship specification, measurement design, empirical cases, comparative synthesis, and provisional theory within one revisable architecture.

## Motivation

Luck is widely invoked in explanations of discovery, innovation, business performance, political change, historical transformation, and individual outcomes. Yet the term often collapses randomness, uncertainty, risk, contingency, unequal exposure, unexpected events, opportunity, surprising outcomes, and moral or social attribution.

The project’s first contribution is analytical discipline: these concepts should be distinguished before their relationships are studied.

## Position Relative to Existing Scholarship

The project draws on, but does not replace:

- philosophical analysis of luck and moral luck;
- statistical and probabilistic treatment of randomness and risk;
- economic work on uncertainty, opportunity, and unequal outcomes;
- psychological research on attribution, judgment, and hindsight;
- historical analysis of contingency and path dependence;
- organizational research on learning, capability, and adaptation;
- political and institutional analysis of power, legitimacy, and selection;
- and complex-systems approaches to interaction and feedback.

Its comparative architecture is a synthesis and research design. Its component concepts are not claimed as individually novel.

## Research Gap

The project addresses four connected gaps.

### Fragmented Concepts

Related constructs are often studied separately or used interchangeably. The project supplies a shared vocabulary while preserving disciplinary differences.

### Outcome and Survivor Selection

Many accounts begin with visible winners, survivors, institutions, discoveries, or technologies. This can turn outcomes into evidence of merit or capability. The project makes selection, unsuccessful cases, and unrealized alternatives central to explanation.

### Weak Ex Ante–Ex Post Separation

Retrospective narratives often make realized paths appear foreseeable. The project separates luck potential, realization processes, and luck attribution.

### Limited Cross-Domain Testing

Similar labels recur across disciplines, but identical terminology does not prove identical mechanisms. The project compares candidate relationships across five domains while requiring domain-specific operationalization and boundary conditions.

## Conceptual Position

The project begins with four working primitives:

1. system;
2. environment;
3. interaction;
4. uncertainty.

Luck is derived rather than primitive. It is an ex post relational evaluation of how consequential uncertainty affected a specified system’s trajectory relative to a stated reference and feasible alternatives.

This rejects luck as a causal force, stochasticity itself, an environmental property, or unexplained residual variance.

## Methodological Position

The project is:

- **relationship-centered:** concepts are nodes; relationships are hypotheses;
- **trajectory-oriented:** states, transitions, sequences, and horizons are separated from outcomes;
- **comparative:** cases span Science, Technology, Business, History, and Politics;
- **selection-aware:** success, survival, and prominence are not sufficient evidence of capability;
- **counterfactual but bounded:** alternatives must have been feasible relative to contemporary information and constraints;
- **method-plural:** tools are selected according to the question;
- **iterative:** theory remains vulnerable to measurement failure, negative cases, and competing explanations.

## Empirical Scope

The five-domain scope is fixed for the current phase:

- Science
- Technology
- Business
- History
- Politics

The cases are analytically selected rather than statistically representative. They are best used for construct development, mechanism discovery, negative-case analysis, and bounded comparison. Strong population-level or universal claims require evidence beyond recurrence in these cases.

## Distinctive Features

The project’s most defensible distinctive features are:

- a four-primitive architecture;
- separation of luck potential, realization, and attribution;
- an opportunity sequence from potential through foreclosure;
- capability–performance and selection–superiority distinctions;
- integration of unrealized opportunities and alternative trajectories;
- a relationship taxonomy spanning constitutive, causal, enabling, constraining, and feedback roles;
- preserved intellectual-history layers alongside additive architectural revisions;
- and a complete research chain from concepts to propositions.

These features constitute a research architecture, not proof that its hypotheses are correct.

## Relationship to Dynamic Social Systems

Research on Luck began after Dynamic Social Systems was tabled following a draft working paper. The projects share interests in systems, institutions, adaptation, feedback, and trajectories, but have different immediate purposes.

Research on Luck should not be treated as an empirical appendix to Dynamic Social Systems, and its architecture should not be transferred automatically. A later cross-project audit should test whether specific distinctions improve Dynamic Social Systems—for example:

- system state versus trajectory;
- capability versus outcome;
- selection versus superiority;
- adaptation versus beneficial change;
- and ex ante uncertainty versus ex post evaluation.

## Claims and Boundaries

The project currently claims that its architecture makes luck attribution and related empirical questions more explicit and testable. It does not yet claim:

- a validated general theory;
- universal mechanisms;
- general causal estimates;
- a single quantitative luck measure;
- reliable prediction of contingent events;
- or normative rankings of institutions or societies.

Its strongest current outputs are conceptual distinctions, research designs, comparative conjectures, and provisional propositions.

## Long-Term Position

The project should be understood as an evolving research program whose quality depends on evidence, discrimination among explanations, and revision.

Its success will be judged by whether it clarifies ambiguous claims, generates bounded hypotheses, improves treatment of selection and alternatives, supports valid measurement, explains realized and unrealized trajectories, and discards propositions that fail.

The goal is not to make luck explain more. It is to make claims involving luck harder to state casually and easier to challenge empirically.
