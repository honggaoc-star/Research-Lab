# Social Inequality

## First Application of the Dynamic Social Systems Framework

This application examines how heterogeneous individuals, institutions, decision rules, historical contingencies, and recursive feedback jointly shape the level and distribution of social outcomes over time.

The central proposition is:

> The evolution of inequality may be as important as its level.

The application studies income, wealth, mobility, education, health, opportunity, political influence, and related outcomes while treating inequality as one observable manifestation of a broader social allocation process.
