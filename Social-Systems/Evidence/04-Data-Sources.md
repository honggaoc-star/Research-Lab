# 04. Data Sources

## Purpose

The Social Systems Framework requires empirical observations to estimate state variables, evaluate theoretical relationships, and test dynamic hypotheses. This document catalogs candidate data sources that may support empirical implementation of the framework.

Rather than prescribing a single preferred dataset, this document provides an inventory of major data resources, summarizes their strengths and limitations, and identifies their potential role within the broader research program.

---

# 1. Principles for Data Selection

Data selection should be guided by several principles.

## Relevance

The dataset should measure variables that closely correspond to the theoretical constructs identified in the framework.

---

## Coverage

Preference should be given to datasets that provide broad coverage across:

- countries,
- regions,
- time periods,
- demographic groups.

---

## Consistency

Definitions should remain reasonably stable across time.

When methodologies change, researchers should document the implications for longitudinal analysis.

---

## Transparency

Data sources should publish:

- methodology,
- definitions,
- revisions,
- limitations.

Transparent documentation improves reproducibility and facilitates independent verification.

# 2. Major International Data Sources

## World Bank – World Development Indicators (WDI)

Primary strengths:

- GDP
- education
- demographics
- labor markets
- public finance
- infrastructure
- health

Potential applications:

- macroeconomic state variables
- development indicators
- cross-country comparisons

Limitations:

- primarily national aggregates
- limited institutional detail

---

## OECD Statistics

Primary strengths:

- advanced economies
- education
- taxation
- productivity
- labor markets
- innovation

Potential applications:

- institutional comparisons
- policy evaluation

Limitations:

- limited coverage outside OECD members

---

## International Monetary Fund (IMF)

Primary strengths:

- fiscal variables
- monetary indicators
- financial statistics
- balance of payments

Potential applications:

- macroeconomic stability
- fiscal capacity
- economic resilience

---

## United Nations Data

Primary strengths:

- demographic statistics
- Sustainable Development Goals
- population projections
- social indicators

Potential applications:

- long-run structural analysis
- international comparisons

# 8. Historical Data

Historical analysis may require specialized sources.

Examples include:

- Maddison Project Database
- historical census archives
- archival fiscal records
- constitutional databases
- historical election data
- legislative archives

Historical evidence is particularly important for evaluating long-term institutional evolution.

---

# 9. Emerging Data Sources

Rapid advances in digital technology provide new opportunities.

Examples include:

- satellite imagery
- remote sensing
- administrative microdata
- transaction data
- social media
- mobility data
- digital governance indicators

These sources offer higher-frequency observations but often introduce privacy, access, and representativeness challenges.

---

# 10. Data Integration

No single dataset captures the complete state of a social system.

Empirical implementation will therefore require integrating multiple sources.

A typical workflow may include:

Theoretical Variable
→ Observable Proxy
→ Dataset
→ Statistical Harmonization
→ Estimated State Variable

This integration process should document:

- source selection,
- temporal alignment,
- geographic harmonization,
- missing observations,
- measurement assumptions.

---

# 11. Future Repository Development
