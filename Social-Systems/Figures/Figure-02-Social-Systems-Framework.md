## Purpose

Figure 2 presents the conceptual architecture of the Social Systems Framework.

It illustrates how a social system may be viewed as a dynamic and adaptive system consisting of interacting components operating within an external environment and evolving through transition mechanisms and adaptive feedback.

The figure provides readers with a high-level conceptual understanding before the mathematical formalization introduced in Chapter 06.

---

## Components Illustrated

The figure includes the following conceptual elements.

### External Environment

Represents exogenous influences including:

- natural environment,
- international conditions,
- technology frontier,
- cultural context,
- external shocks.

---

### Social System

The central analytical object.

The current version illustrates six principal components:

- Individuals
- Institutions
- Human Capital
- Political System
- Economy
- Technology

These components interact continuously through multiple feedback processes.

---

### Adaptive Feedback

Illustrates recursive interactions among system components.

Feedback represents learning, adaptation, institutional adjustment, and behavioral responses that modify future system evolution.

---

### Transition Mechanisms

Represents the collection of mechanisms responsible for transforming one system state into another.

Examples include:

- institutional adaptation,
- behavioral adjustment,
- political change,
- technological innovation,
- demographic transition,
- resource reallocation.

---

### Social Outcomes

Illustrative outcomes include:

- well-being,
- equity,
- economic performance,
- stability,
- legitimacy,
- sustainability.

These outcomes become inputs into future system evolution through adaptive feedback.

---

### Updated System State

Represents the new state of the social system following one cycle of evolution.

The updated state becomes the starting point for the next transition.

---

## Scientific Role

This figure serves as the principal conceptual illustration of the Social Systems Framework.

Its purpose is to communicate the architecture of the framework before introducing mathematical notation.

It should therefore be interpreted as a conceptual model rather than a mathematical diagram.
