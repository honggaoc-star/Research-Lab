## Purpose

Figure 03 translates the conceptual architecture presented in Figure 02 into a mathematical framework.

Rather than focusing on individual social components, it represents the evolution of the entire system through a sequence of system states connected by a transition operator. The figure provides readers with an intuitive understanding of the mathematical model before formal notation and equations are introduced.

---

## Components Illustrated

### External Environment

Represented by **E(t)**.

External environmental conditions influence the evolution of the social system but are not fully determined by the system itself. Examples include international developments, natural events, technological frontiers, and external economic shocks.

---

### Current System State

Represented by **S(t)**.

The state vector summarizes the condition of the social system at time *t*. It includes the principal state variables that characterize the system, such as individuals, institutions, human capital, political organization, economic activity, and technological capability.

---

### Transition Operator

Represented by **T(·)**.

The transition operator maps one system state into the next according to the governing dynamics of the framework.

Conceptually,

S(t+1) = T(S(t), E(t))

where both the current system state and external environment influence future evolution.

---

### Updated System State

Represented by **S(t+1)**.

The updated state becomes the starting point for the next stage of system evolution.

---

### Observable Social Outcomes

Observable outcomes emerge from the evolving system state.

Examples include:

- Economic growth
- Inequality
- Social mobility
- Well-being
- Political stability
- Innovation

These outcomes provide the empirical basis for testing the framework.

---

### Adaptive Feedback

Observable outcomes influence future system evolution through adaptive feedback.

Feedback represents learning, institutional adjustment, behavioral adaptation, policy responses, and other recursive mechanisms that modify future state transitions.
