# 01. Problem Formulation

## Purpose

This document defines the scientific problem addressed by the **Adaptive
Intervention Dynamics** workstream.

It establishes the object of study, the central research question,
conceptual boundaries, and the rationale for treating intervention
effectiveness as an endogenous property of dynamic social systems.

No mathematical model is introduced here. The objective is to define the
problem precisely before selecting an appropriate formal representation.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 1. Motivation

Social systems are continually influenced by deliberate interventions.

Examples include:

-   public policies
-   institutional reforms
-   organizational strategies
-   technological deployments
-   educational programs
-   regulatory changes
-   public health initiatives

These interventions are intended to influence future system behavior.

However, observed effectiveness often changes over time. Some
interventions become less effective, some improve through institutional
learning, while others produce delayed, nonlinear, or unintended
consequences.

The common feature is that intervention effectiveness is not generally
constant.

Understanding why effectiveness changes is therefore a scientific
problem rather than simply an empirical observation.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 2. The Scientific Problem

Traditional policy evaluation frequently treats intervention
effectiveness as a fixed parameter.

The Dynamic Social Systems Framework adopts a different perspective.

Interventions alter the social system. The altered social system
subsequently changes the effectiveness of future interventions.

Intervention effectiveness therefore becomes part of the evolving system
rather than an external constant.

This recursive relationship motivates the Adaptive Intervention Dynamics
workstream.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 3. Central Research Question

> **How does the adaptive evolution of a social system influence the
> effectiveness of interventions over time?**

Subordinate questions include:

-   Which mechanisms generate changing effectiveness?
-   Under what conditions does effectiveness decline?
-   Under what conditions can effectiveness improve?
-   Which adaptive mechanisms dominate different classes of
    intervention?
-   Can intervention robustness be characterized formally?

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 4. Object of Study

The workstream studies the dynamics of intervention effectiveness rather
than any specific intervention.

Accordingly, the object of study is:

> **The relationship between an intervention and the evolving social
> system in which that intervention operates.**

The focus is therefore on:

-   adaptive behavior
-   institutional learning
-   recursive feedback
-   strategic response
-   endogenous adjustment

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 5. Scope

## Included

-   intervention effectiveness
-   behavioral adaptation
-   institutional adaptation
-   recursive policy feedback
-   changing system response
-   robustness under adaptation

## Excluded

-   welfare evaluation
-   ethical analysis
-   political desirability
-   optimal policy design
-   implementation strategy

These belong to separate normative or application-specific studies.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 6. Relationship to Other Formalization Tracks

**Institutional Translation** explains how institutions transform
heterogeneous characteristics into outcomes.

**Endogenous Transition Dynamics** explains how transition probabilities
evolve.

**Adaptive Intervention Dynamics** explains how the effectiveness of
interventions evolves as the system itself changes.

The three workstreams therefore address complementary aspects of the
same dynamic social system.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 7. Expected Contributions

This workstream seeks to provide:

-   a general conceptual framework for adaptive intervention
-   bounded mathematical representations of intervention dynamics
-   testable propositions concerning changing effectiveness
-   formal links between intervention design and system evolution
-   integration with the broader Dynamic Social Systems Framework

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 8. Criteria for Success

The workstream will be considered successful if it produces a bounded
theory that:

-   explains why intervention effectiveness changes
-   identifies explicit adaptive mechanisms
-   generates observable predictions
-   distinguishes itself from fixed-parameter intervention models
-   can be subjected to empirical testing and potential falsification
