# 02. Conceptual Mechanisms

## Purpose

This document identifies the principal mechanisms through which the
effectiveness of interventions changes over time.

The objective is to establish a conceptual taxonomy before introducing
mathematical representations.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 1. Why Mechanisms Matter

An intervention does not operate in isolation.

It changes the social system, and the resulting changes influence the
effectiveness of subsequent interventions.

The central task is therefore to identify the mechanisms responsible for
this recursive adaptation.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 2. Mechanism Taxonomy

## 2.1 Behavioral Adaptation

Individuals and organizations adjust their behavior in response to
interventions.

Examples include learning, avoidance, strategic compliance,
substitution, and innovation.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 2.2 Institutional Adaptation

Institutions revise rules, procedures, enforcement, and governance in
response to observed outcomes.

Institutional learning may either strengthen or weaken intervention
effectiveness.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 2.3 Strategic Interaction

Actors anticipate interventions and modify decisions before or after
implementation.

Adaptive expectations may substantially alter intervention outcomes.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 2.4 Technological Adaptation

Technological change creates new opportunities, constraints, and
unintended responses that modify intervention performance.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 2.5 Political Feedback

Observed outcomes influence public opinion, political coalitions,
administrative priorities, and future policy choices.

Interventions may therefore alter the political environment governing
later interventions.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 2.6 Environmental Change

External shocks, demographic shifts, economic conditions, and
international developments may change the context in which an
intervention operates.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 3. Recursive Feedback

These mechanisms are not independent.

Behavior changes institutions.

Institutions alter incentives.

Incentives reshape behavior.

The resulting outcomes generate additional rounds of adaptation.

Adaptive Intervention Dynamics therefore studies recursive rather than
one-time causal effects.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 4. Conceptual Representation

At the conceptual level:

Intervention

↓

Adaptive mechanisms

↓

Changed system response

↓

Changed intervention effectiveness

↓

Future intervention decisions

This recursive loop distinguishes Adaptive Intervention Dynamics from
static intervention analysis.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 5. Design Principles

Mechanisms included in later models should:

-   have clear conceptual definitions;
-   generate observable implications;
-   be distinguishable from one another;
-   permit empirical testing;
-   remain compatible with the Dynamic Social Systems Framework.

Mechanisms should not be added solely to improve model fit.
