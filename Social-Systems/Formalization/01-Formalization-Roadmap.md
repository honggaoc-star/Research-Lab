# Formalization Roadmap

## Phase VI — Mathematical and Computational Development of the Dynamic Social Systems Framework

**Repository**

`Research-Lab/Social-Systems/Formalization/01-Formalization-Roadmap.md`

**Status**

Working Draft

**Phase**

VI — Formalization

**Purpose**

To define the objectives, scope, sequence, and guiding principles for translating the validated Dynamic Social Systems framework into explicit mathematical and computational models.

---

# 1. Purpose of the Formalization Phase

The preceding phases established the conceptual foundation of the Dynamic Social Systems research program.

They answered four major questions.

## Framework

> What is the conceptual architecture?

---

## Literature

> How does the framework relate to existing scholarship?

---

## Validation

> Which claims survive critical evaluation?

---

## Repository Development

> How should the research program be organized?

The present phase asks a different question.

> Can the validated framework be translated into mathematically precise, operationally useful, and empirically testable models?

The objective is no longer conceptual expansion.

The objective is scientific demonstration.

---

# 2. Guiding Principles

Formalization should follow several principles established during Validation.

## Principle 1 — Preserve the Conceptual Freeze

The four foundational axioms remain provisionally frozen.

No new foundational concept should be introduced unless formalization demonstrates that the existing architecture cannot function without it.

---

## Principle 2 — Formalize One Mechanism at a Time

The Dynamic Social Systems framework contains many interacting mechanisms.

The first models should isolate one mechanism rather than attempting to formalize the entire architecture simultaneously.

---

## Principle 3 — Minimum Sufficient Representation

Every model should use the smallest state vector capable of answering the selected research question.

Additional variables require explicit justification.

---

## Principle 4 — Maintain Positive–Normative Separation

Formal models should explain how social systems evolve.

They should not implicitly determine which outcomes are socially desirable.

Normative analysis requires explicit welfare assumptions.

---

## Principle 5 — Benchmark Every Model

Each model should be compared with a simpler alternative.

The framework should earn additional complexity through improved explanatory or predictive value.

---

## Principle 6 — Treat Failure as Information

Formalization is expected to reveal weaknesses.

A failed model should lead to refinement, narrowing, or rejection rather than immediate expansion of the architecture.

---

# 3. Objectives of Formalization

The Formalization phase has six primary objectives.

## Objective A

Translate conceptual mechanisms into explicit mathematical relationships.

---

## Objective B

Identify the minimum sufficient state representation required for each model.

---

## Objective C

Generate distinctive predictions that differ from neighboring theories.

---

## Objective D

Produce models suitable for historical and empirical testing.

---

## Objective E

Clarify which mechanisms are essential and which are application-specific.

---

## Objective F

Determine whether the framework provides analytical value beyond conceptual integration.

# 5. Scope of Formalization

The Dynamic Social Systems framework is broader than any individual model.

Formalization therefore proceeds from the general framework toward progressively more specific representations.

The sequence is:

```text
General Framework
        ↓
Conceptual Architecture
        ↓
Validated Mechanisms
        ↓
Reduced Models
        ↓
Historical / Empirical Testing
```

No single mathematical model is expected to represent the complete framework.

---

# 6. Initial Formalization Program

The Validation phase identified three mechanisms with the greatest potential scientific value.

## Track 1 — Institutional Translation

Research Question:

> How do institutions alter the mapping from heterogeneous actor characteristics to social outcomes?

Primary proposition:

Institutions modify the relationship between heterogeneity and observed outcomes.

---

## Track 2 — Endogenous Transition Dynamics

Research Question:

> Why do transition probabilities themselves evolve?

Primary proposition:

Institutional, behavioral, political, and distributional conditions alter future transition dynamics.

This represents the strongest candidate for a distinctive theoretical contribution.

---

## Track 3 — Adaptive Intervention Dynamics

Research Question:

> How does behavioral adaptation alter intervention effectiveness, and how do institutions respond?

Primary proposition:

Intervention effectiveness evolves through recursive interaction between behavioral adaptation and institutional learning.

# 8. Formalization Artifacts

## 02 — Mathematical Notation

Establish consistent notation for:

* states;
* actors;
* institutions;
* mechanisms;
* observables;
* shocks;
* parameters.

This prevents notation drift across later models.

---

## 03 — Minimum State Vector

Determine the smallest state representation capable of supporting the first generation of models.

This document is expected to be one of the most important in the entire research program.

---

## 04 — Institutional Translation

Construct the first explicit model linking:

* heterogeneous actors;
* institutional rules;
* observed outcomes.

---

## 05 — Endogenous Transition Dynamics

Develop the framework's central dynamic contribution.

Rather than treating transition probabilities as fixed, model how institutions and feedback modify them.

---

## 06 — Adaptive Intervention Dynamics

Formalize institutional intervention, behavioral adaptation, institutional learning, and changing policy effectiveness.

---

## 07 — Integrated Dynamic Model

Combine the previous components into a unified but still bounded dynamic framework.

This document should integrate—not replace—the earlier models.

---

## 08 — Formalization Summary

Evaluate:

* mathematical consistency;
* explanatory value;
* remaining weaknesses;
* readiness for empirical implementation.

---

# 9. Modeling Philosophy

Formalization should emphasize clarity over sophistication.

The preferred order is:

1. conceptual clarity;
2. mathematical precision;
3. analytical tractability;
4. empirical usefulness.

Not:

1. mathematical complexity;
2. analytical elegance;
3. empirical interpretation.

Complex mathematics should appear only when required.

---

# 10. Minimum Modeling Requirements

Every formal model should identify:

* research question;
* system boundary;
* actors;
* state variables;
* institutional variables;
* transition mechanisms;
* behavioral assumptions;
* shocks;
* observables;
* propositions;
* benchmark;
* falsification conditions.

Models missing any of these elements should not be considered complete.

---

# 11. Relationship to Evidence

Formalization and Evidence should evolve together.

```text
Formal Model
        ↓
Predictions
        ↓
Historical / Empirical Tests
        ↓
Framework Revision
```

The purpose of formalization is not mathematical elegance alone.

Its purpose is to generate propositions that can survive confrontation with evidence.

---

# 12. Relationship to Applications

Applications should not drive the general framework.

Instead:

```text
Framework
        ↓
Formal Model
        ↓
Application
```

The first application remains:

**Social Inequality**

Future applications should reuse the same architecture whenever possible.

---

# 13. Success Criteria

The Formalization phase will be considered successful when it produces:

* one coherent mathematical language;
* one minimum sufficient state vector;
* one operational representation of Institutional Translation;
* one operational representation of Endogenous Transition Dynamics;
* one operational representation of Adaptive Intervention Dynamics;
* one integrated reduced model;
* several distinctive predictions;
* explicit links to historical and empirical testing.

---

# 14. Failure Criteria

Formalization should be reconsidered if:

* no minimum sufficient state vector can be identified;
* every model requires nearly the full conceptual architecture;
* Institutional Translation cannot be operationalized;
* transition dynamics cannot be distinguished from existing models;
* additional mathematics produces no additional explanation;
* models become application-specific without preserving a common framework.

These outcomes would indicate that the conceptual architecture requires further refinement before continued formalization.

# 16. Current Priority

The Validation phase concluded that the framework's strongest remaining candidate contribution is:

> **Endogenous Transition Dynamics**

Nevertheless, the Formalization phase should not begin there.

The correct starting point is:

> **02-Mathematical-Notation.md**

A common mathematical language must be established before introducing formal models.

Notation chosen early will propagate through every later equation, proof, simulation, empirical specification, and manuscript.

Investing effort in a coherent notation now will greatly simplify the remainder of the research program.

---

# Preliminary Conclusion

The Formalization phase represents the transition from conceptual architecture to scientific modeling.

Its purpose is not to increase the scope of the Dynamic Social Systems framework but to determine whether the validated architecture can generate explicit, tractable, falsifiable, and empirically meaningful models.

Formalization should proceed incrementally through a sequence of bounded models, beginning with common notation and a minimum sufficient state vector before addressing Institutional Translation, Endogenous Transition Dynamics, and Adaptive Intervention Dynamics.

Success will be measured not by mathematical sophistication, but by whether the resulting models improve explanation, generate distinctive predictions, survive comparison with simpler alternatives, and connect naturally to historical and empirical evidence.
