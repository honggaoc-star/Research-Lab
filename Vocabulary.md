# Vocabulary v1.0

## A Common Conceptual Vocabulary for the Research Lab

---

# Purpose

The Research Lab investigates complex adaptive systems through multiple independent research programs.

As repositories mature, recurring concepts increasingly appear across disciplines and domains.

This document establishes a shared conceptual vocabulary to promote consistency, clarity, and cumulative scientific understanding throughout the Lab.

The vocabulary defines concepts rather than ordinary language.

Definitions may evolve as additional evidence becomes available.

---

# Foundational Concepts

## Adaptive System

A collection of interacting components capable of changing their behavior or structure in response to internal dynamics, external conditions, or accumulated experience.

Adaptive systems may include individuals, organizations, markets, institutions, ecosystems, or societies.

---

## Agent

An entity capable of perceiving information, making decisions, and taking actions within an environment.

Agents may be individuals, organizations, institutions, artificial systems, or other decision-making entities.

---

## Environment

The external conditions, constraints, opportunities, and interactions surrounding an adaptive system or agent.

Environments influence adaptation while simultaneously being modified by adaptive systems.

---

## Institution

A relatively stable set of formal or informal rules, norms, incentives, or organizational structures that shape behavior and interactions within a system.

Institutions evolve over time and both constrain and enable adaptation.

---

## Mechanism

A recurring process or causal structure through which change occurs.

Mechanisms explain how outcomes arise rather than merely describing observed relationships.

Mechanisms are expected to recur across multiple independent domains.

---

# Dynamic Concepts

## Adaptation

The process through which an adaptive system changes its behavior, structure, or strategy in response to changing conditions.

Adaptation may improve, reduce, or simply alter system performance.

---

## Evolution

Long-term cumulative change resulting from repeated adaptation, selection, interaction, and historical development.

Evolution may occur within biological, technological, institutional, economic, or social systems.

---

## Feedback

A process in which the consequences of previous actions influence future behavior or system dynamics.

Feedback may reinforce (positive feedback) or stabilize (negative feedback) subsequent system evolution.

---

## Path Dependence

The property whereby previous decisions, events, or states constrain or influence future possibilities.

Historical trajectories therefore affect subsequent development.

---

## Emergence

System-level properties or behaviors arising from interactions among components that cannot be fully understood by examining individual components alone.

---

# Uncertainty Concepts

## Uncertainty

Incomplete knowledge regarding future states, outcomes, or causal relationships.

Uncertainty may arise from limited information, inherent variability, or system complexity.

---

## Risk

Uncertainty for which meaningful probabilities or likelihoods can be reasonably estimated.

---

## Stochasticity

Variation resulting from probabilistic processes or events.

The term *stochastic* is preferred over *random* when discussing scientific mechanisms because it encompasses probabilistic structure without implying complete absence of underlying processes.

---

## Luck

The influence of stochastic events whose consequences depend upon subsequent recognition, interpretation, adaptation, and action.

Luck is treated as a mechanism influencing opportunity rather than as a complete explanation of outcomes.

---

## Chance

A general descriptive term referring to probabilistic occurrence.

Within the Research Lab, *chance* should not be used interchangeably with *luck* or *stochasticity* unless context specifically requires it.

---

# Opportunity Concepts

## Opportunity

A set of future possibilities whose realization depends upon subsequent recognition, interpretation, decision, and action under uncertainty.

Opportunity represents a dynamic property rather than a guaranteed outcome.

---

## Opportunity Recognition

Identification of previously unnoticed or newly emerging opportunities.

Recognition does not ensure realization.

---

## Opportunity Translation

Transformation of scientific or conceptual knowledge into practical capability.

(Currently supported within the Technology domain.)

---

## Opportunity Integration

Combination of multiple capabilities into a coherent system.

(Currently supported within the Technology domain.)

---

## Opportunity Interconnection

Creation of broader infrastructures by linking previously independent systems.

(Currently supported within the Technology domain.)

---

## Opportunity Democratization

Expansion of access to existing opportunities through reduced barriers to participation.

(Currently supported within the Technology domain.)

---

## Opportunity Repurposing

Application of existing capabilities or infrastructures to new domains beyond their original purpose.

(Currently supported within the Technology domain.)

---

## Opportunity Orchestration

Coordination of multiple mature opportunities into adaptive platforms capable of generating continuing innovation.

(Currently supported within the Technology domain.)

---

# Research Concepts

## Repository

A long-term research program investigating a coherent scientific question.

Repositories are living research environments rather than static publications.

---

## Research Program

A coordinated body of research focused on a major scientific question.

Within the Research Lab, repositories function as research programs.

---

## General Theory

The current synthesis of evidence accumulated within a repository.

General theories remain provisional and are expected to evolve through continued empirical investigation.

---

## Comparative Synthesis

The systematic comparison of evidence across multiple case studies or domains in order to identify recurring mechanisms and refine theoretical understanding.

---

## Research Observation

An evidence-informed insight, unexpected pattern, unresolved question, or emerging hypothesis recorded during ongoing investigation.

Research observations motivate further inquiry but do not constitute established conclusions.

---

# Preferred Usage

The following conventions should be used consistently throughout the Research Lab.

Prefer:

* Adaptive system
* Mechanism
* Opportunity
* Stochastic event
* Repository
* Research program
* General Theory
* Comparative Synthesis
* Research Observation

Avoid when greater precision is required:

* Random event
* Final theory
* Project (for mature repositories)
* Discovery (when referring to evolving opportunity)
* Chance and luck as interchangeable terms

---

# Living Vocabulary

Vocabulary v1.0 represents the current conceptual language of the Research Lab.

As repositories mature, definitions may be refined, new concepts may be introduced, and obsolete terminology may be retired.

Changes should remain evidence-driven and documented through version updates so that the evolution of the Lab's conceptual framework remains transparent and scientifically traceable.
